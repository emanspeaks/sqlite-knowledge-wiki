# The handbook's reading

What NASA-HDBK-2203 says each requirement asks for in practice: the objective evidence of compliance, the documents that hold it, and what software assurance does. The handbook is guidance; the requirements are the NPR's.

## SWE-013 Software Plans

%guidance[HB-013 interprets=SWE-013] The project manager develops, baselines, maintains and executes software plans, including security plans, that cover the whole life cycle and address the NPR requirements with approved tailoring. Plans must be complete, correct, workable, consistent and verifiable; they are baselined before they are implemented, and revisions are reviewed and approved before use (approval need not wait for a milestone review) and distributed to affected stakeholders. The project manager is responsible; a software or development lead may see to execution and maintenance; plans are updated when scope, requirements, estimates, resources, schedules, risks, budgets, regulations or stakeholder commitments change, and adherence is checked through plan tracking (SWE-024), SA and audits.

- **Objective evidence:** signed or approved software plans covering the full life cycle (SDP/SMP, SCMP, test plans, SAP, software safety plan if safety-critical, IT security plan); tailoring rationale and approval records; software classification justification; records of tailored requirements removed, adjusted or combined; plan consistency review reports; traceability matrix from requirements to plans; maturity assessment reports at milestones; plan version control records and change logs with approval signatures; plan review and approval records (minutes, sign-offs, audit results); risk records for plan omissions or conflicts; SA Tasking Checklist Tool outputs; records of SA activities (verification, validation, security testing, compliance audits); test reports and defect logs; CM baseline approval and audit records; security testing results (static and dynamic analysis, penetration tests); threat modeling and vulnerability mitigation records; post-deployment security monitoring and patch logs; SA personnel training and qualification records; independent oversight review records; SMA reporting chain records
- **Documents:** 5.08 SDP-SMP; 5.06 SCMP; 5.10 STP; 5.17 Software Assurance Plan Minimum Content; 5.18 Safety Plan Minimum Content; 8.51 Software Assurance Plan; software maintenance plan; IT security plan; 7.18 Documentation Guidance (recommended plans and content); 7.05 WBS that includes software
- **Milestones:** Plans and progress against them are reviewed at life cycle milestone reviews; for space flight projects the expected maturity of each plan (draft, preliminary, baselined, updated, final) at each review is given in the 7.08 matrix.
- **Small projects:** Tailor the plans to size and criticality; the SDP/SMP, SAP, SCMP, security plan and maintenance plan may be combined into one integrated plan as long as assurance and security are fully addressed. Tailoring records name the approving authority, what was tailored out and why, and plans are reviewed and approved (project manager, SA lead, risk or security officer) before implementation.
- **Software assurance:** confirm all plans, including security plans, are in place with expected content for the life cycle events and proper tailoring for the software class (8739.8B task 1); develop and maintain a Software Assurance Plan per NASA-HDBK-2203 content, including software safety (task 2); SA products: plan review checklists and reports, SAP and its update log, issue and risk records, compliance tracking and gap analysis reports, tailoring assessment reports
- **Also:** Minimum plan set named in the guidance: software development or management plan, SCM plan, test plans, maintenance plans, SA plans, software safety plan (if safety-critical). SA plans should be built from the SA Tasking Checklist Tool (8.15), which tailors SA tasks by class, safety-criticality and milestones and exports to Excel, CSV, XML (JIRA, MS Project). The SAP must name who (NASA, contractor, Center) performs each assurance activity, and SA keeps a reporting chain independent of development. Citing CMMI, criteria for a significant deviation from plan should be set, saying when and by whom they are applied. Center PALs (SPAN) hold plan templates.

## SWE-015 Cost Estimation

%guidance[HB-015 interprets=SWE-015] The project manager establishes, documents and maintains software cost estimate models with their parameters: two models for Class A and B projects estimated at $2 million or more, one model for Class A and B under $2 million and for Classes C, D and F. The estimating process is documented (inputs, assumptions, methods, uncertainties, supporting data), uses scoping, a WBS-based bottom-up estimate and parametric models (COCOMO II, SEER-SEM), is peer reviewed against the BOE and WBS, formally approved by project and software managers, configuration managed and updated through the life cycle as scope and requirements change.

- **Objective evidence:** documents showing the required number of cost estimate models for the class and cost threshold; repository records or archived emails confirming estimate submission; cost estimates showing full life cycle coverage, selected project attributes (size, reuse, modified code, risk), technology costs, SA and software safety costs, and direct costs (training, procurement); review notes or reports evaluating the SWE-151 conditions; records that SA cost estimates were created, verified and included in the overall cost model
- **Documents:** software cost estimate and basis of estimate (BOE) with WBS; project and SA repositories; cost repository (SWE-142)
- **Milestones:** SA confirms estimates are submitted and updated after major milestones (for example SDR, PDR, CDR).
- **Small projects:** Same principles at smaller scale: analogy-based estimates from historical data, or effort-based estimates from expert judgment when no history exists; calibrate any parametric model for small efforts, keep assumptions documented, and keep testing, rework and risk costs in the estimate.
- **Software assurance:** confirm the required number of software cost estimates are complete and include SA cost estimates, including the cost of handling safety-critical software and data (8739.8B task 1); independently estimate SA and software safety cost (grassroots or percentage based, for example 5 to 6 percent of software cost) and check it is in the project model; confirm estimates meet SWE-151 conditions and track missing items to closure; SA products: SA cost estimate report, cost estimate completeness assessment report, SWE-151 compliance assessment
- **Also:** Reused code needing more than 50 percent modification is treated as new code for estimating; count size with a tool such as SLiC, separating new, reused and modified code. One of the two estimates should ideally be model based (parametric or analogy). Do not cut cost by removing reserves or assuming optimistic inheritance; on a mismatch with budget, reduce scope instead. Update the project cost repository with size, effort and schedule data (SWE-142). Related activity: A.01 Software Life Cycle Planning.

## SWE-016 Software Schedule

%guidance[HB-016 interprets=SWE-016] The project manager documents and maintains a software schedule that is coordinated with the overall project schedule, shows milestone and deliverable interactions among software, hardware, operations and the rest of the system, reflects critical dependencies, and accounts for other-project and cross-program dependencies. It is built from a software WBS with milestones, constraints, task sequencing, slack and resources, baselined with management approval and documented in the SDP/SMP, then kept current through status reporting, impact evaluation of changes, critical path analysis and margin monitoring.

- **Objective evidence:** complete software schedule with milestones for all software activities, SA audits, reviews and analyses, dependencies and slack; approved SA and software engineering plans cross-referencing project schedules and dependencies; schedule assessment reports showing alignment with project milestones and issues found; dependency mapping and tracking records; risk logs for schedule misalignment and dependency risks
- **Documents:** 5.08 SDP-SMP (schedule baseline documented there); 5.05 Software Metrics Report; 8.51 Software Assurance Plan (SA schedule)
- **Milestones:** Schedule includes major reviews such as PDR, CDR and TRR, aligned to system life cycle reviews per NASA/SP-2010-3403; plan maturity by review per 7.08.
- **Small projects:** Tailor tools and detail to scale: Excel, Word or text schedules are often enough; use Microsoft Project or OmniPlan when the schedule must integrate with larger efforts or manage dependencies.
- **Software assurance:** assess that the software schedule satisfies the conditions of the requirement (8739.8B task 1); develop a software assurance schedule including SA products, audits, reporting and reviews (task 2); SA products: SA schedule, SAP, SA schedule assessment report, software engineering plan assessment, SA risk management plan and logs
- **Also:** Schedule elements: planned activities mapped to the SDP, milestones and deliverables (including documentation, CM and assurance deliverables), critical dependencies with critical path analysis, external program inputs (APIs, libraries, tools) with contingencies. Suggested SA schedule items: SA plan, audits, status reports, SA and safety requirements mapping table, SA cost estimate, IV&V planning and execution plan if required, hazard analysis assessment, software safety analysis, independent static code analysis for cybersecurity and secure coding, requirements and design analysis, SA peer reviews, metric reporting, status reviews. Tools named: Microsoft Project, Primavera, OmniPlan, Gantt and PERT charts.

## SWE-017 Project and Software Training

%guidance[HB-017 interprets=SWE-017] The project manager plans, tracks and ensures project-specific software training for project personnel, including assigned software assurance personnel (NPR note). Starting in the Formulation phase, the team assesses needed skills and gaps (surveys, interviews, past projects), records training needs and plans in the SDP/SMP (or justifies the absence of training needs), feeds the Center training plan, then schedules, records and evaluates training (surveys, lessons learned, performance) and closes gaps.

- **Objective evidence:** training schedules or plans showing planned software training; SDP/SMP or SAP sections identifying required training topics for software and SA personnel; training logs with type of training, completion dates and hours; STEP or SATERN transcripts for SA personnel (Level 1 to 4 progress); project orientation materials covering system requirements, concept of operations and goals
- **Documents:** 5.08 SDP-SMP (training section); 5.15 Software Training Plan; Center training plan; SAP; SATERN records
- **Milestones:** Training planning begins in the Formulation phase as software requirements are defined.
- **Small projects:** Reuse training plans from similar earlier projects where requirements, tools and methods match, or write an umbrella training plan covering a family of similar projects, tailored to the project's needs.
- **Software assurance:** confirm project-specific software training has been planned, tracked and completed for project personnel, including SA and software safety personnel (8739.8B task 1); confirm SA and software safety personnel have completed appropriate SA or safety training (task 2); SA products: SA training plan compliance report, SA personnel training records, SA professional development plan evaluation, training gaps and risk assessment report
- **Also:** Training areas to assess: architecture and design, process methods, requirements, CM, languages and environments, target systems (RTOS, cFE/cFS), V&V, software assurance, plus project management and domain knowledge. Sources: OCE, APPEL, Center SEPG, STEP (SMA training), Center training officers; SATERN for scheduling and tracking. A benchmark of 40 training hours per year is cited. SA discipline training (Intermediate Software Assurance, Agile, Software Testing) should appear in SA training plans.

## SWE-018 Software Activities Review

%guidance[HB-018 interprets=SWE-018] The project manager regularly reviews software schedule activities, status, performance metrics and assessment or analysis results with project stakeholders and tracks resulting issues to closure. Major life cycle reviews are event driven (held when entrance criteria are met), with interim reviews (weekly, monthly or per phase) sized to the project, class and safety-criticality; the review schedule is documented in the SDP/SMP and Center procedures take precedence. Unresolved issues are logged with owners and timelines in a risk system, PRACA or CM tool consistent with the CM plan and revisited at later reviews.

- **Objective evidence:** meeting agendas and invitations showing regular reviews; stakeholder attendance records; minutes with decisions, actions and follow-ups; review frequency logs; updated schedules (Gantt charts, timelines) presented; critical path analysis reports; performance metrics reports (SV, SPI, milestone completion, testing status); schedule risk reports and contingency plans; metrics presentations and schedule health reports; issue tracking logs (description, priority, owner, status, due date, schedule impact); records of schedule changes from resolved issues; issue closure and escalation records; stakeholder feedback and consensus records; review slide decks; updated baseline documentation; action tracker closure; compliance and audit reports; review improvement logs
- **Documents:** 5.08 SDP-SMP (review schedule); 5.06 SCMP; 8.52 Software Assurance Status Reports; issue or PRACA tracking system
- **Milestones:** Major milestone reviews such as PDR and CDR when entrance criteria are met (7.09); product maturity per 7.08; Key Decision Points assess technical maturity, resources and internal factors; external stakeholders normally attend formal milestone reviews only.
- **Small projects:** Small, lower-risk projects may justify less frequent reviews based on software class (SWE-020) and a risk assessment, using 7.08 and Center procedures, and re-evaluating class and risk periodically.
- **Software assurance:** confirm generation and distribution of periodic reports on software schedule activities, metrics and status, including SA and software safety schedule activities, metrics and status (8739.8B task 1); confirm closure of project software schedule issues (task 2); SA product: lists of non-conformances found in reviews with corrective action status
- **Also:** Review content: development tasks (planning through test results), entrance and exit evidence, metrics from the measurement process, risk updates (SWE-086) and problem status. Internal stakeholders: QA, systems engineering, operations, IV&V, independent test, SA, management and engineering. The SA status report (8.52) content: title and date, stoplight dashboard, contributions, current and slated tasks, issue tracking, metrics, process improvement, obstacles and watch items, risk summary, funding, schedule; its content is pre-coordinated in the SAP. Evidence should be traceable to specific reviews and kept under CM.

## SWE-020 Software Classification

%guidance[HB-020 interprets=SWE-020] The project manager classifies every system and subsystem containing software at the highest applicable class (A to F) from NPR 7150.2 Appendix D, as soon as it is known the project includes software. Classification weighs usage, system criticality, human dependence, developmental and operational complexity and Agency investment; Appendix C (Requirements Mapping Matrix) with the safety-critical designation then sets which requirements apply. The software engineering and SMA technical authorities must agree (SA either classifies independently or concurs), disagreements go through the Center dissenting opinion process, records are kept by the project, and the class is revisited at major reviews or when design, operational use or scope changes.

- **Objective evidence:** classification assignments for all software systems and subsystems; classification rationale tied to Appendix D definitions; safety-critical designations per NASA-STD-8739.8; signed SA and engineering classification agreement; minutes or communications of classification discussions; discrepancy reports or notifications to the ETA and SMA TA chains if disagreement; resolution records and resulting classification changes
- **Documents:** 5.08 SDP-SMP; 8.51 Software Assurance Plan; 7.16 Appendix C Requirements Mapping and Compliance Matrix; requirements compliance matrix (SWE-125); software records (SWE-176)
- **Milestones:** Classify as soon as the project includes software; revalidate at major reviews such as PDR and CDR; SA metric tracks discrepancies resolved before major reviews.
- **Small projects:** Reuse or reference classification decisions from similar earlier projects (similar technologies, methods and risk profiles), use a simplified workflow, classify early and keep documentation concise.
- **Software assurance:** perform a software classification or concur with the engineering classification per NPR 7150.2 (8739.8B task 1); SA products: software classification assessment report, evidence of SA concurrence, software classification discrepancy report if applicable; escalate unresolved disagreements through the ETA and SMA TA chains
- **Also:** Misclassifying to reduce requirements is prohibited; if requirements cannot be met, use a waiver through the Engineering Technical Authority. Moving to a higher class (for example research Class C becoming operational Class B) follows SWE-021 and 7.13, with the compliance matrix (SWE-125) updated. The page reproduces the Appendix D definitions, examples and exclusions for each class (A human-rated space; B non-human space or large-scale aeronautics over $250M life cycle cost; C mission support, smaller aeronautics, major facilities, CubeSats, SmallSats, sounding rockets, Class D payloads; D basic science and engineering tools; E design concept and research; F general purpose, business and IT). Centers may require an independent SA classification.

## SWE-022 Software Assurance

%guidance[HB-022 interprets=SWE-022] The project manager plans and implements software assurance, software safety and IV&V (if required) per NASA-STD-8739.8 throughout the life of the project; some analyses may be done by engineering or the project (NPR note). In practice the project, with SMA, writes a Software Assurance Plan scaled to class, scope and risk, provides people, tools, facilities and budget, carries out SA activities in every phase, holds audits, tracks metrics, and acts on findings until closure. Tailoring is documented in the SAP with rationale and risk acceptance by the project manager and approval by the SMA technical authority.

- **Objective evidence:** Software Assurance Plan mapped to NASA-STD-8739.8; software safety plan; IV&V implementation plan if applicable; requirements mapping or traceability matrix of SA and safety requirements; risk management plan; process assessment and audit reports; software hazard analyses (FMEA, FTA); IV&V progress reports; safety-critical software test results (nominal and off-nominal); code inspection and static or dynamic analysis logs; cybersecurity testing results; CM records for assurance plans and artifacts; V&V strategy reports; safety-critical validation reports; IV&V final report if applicable; signed certification of compliance with NASA-STD-8739.8; post-deployment anomaly logs; maintenance and sustainment reports
- **Documents:** 8.51 Software Assurance Plan; software safety plan; IV&V plan; requirements mapping matrix; 5.05 Software Metrics Report; 8.59 Audit Reports; risk register
- **Milestones:** SA audits at least once per life cycle milestone, or at least every two years for long projects or on request of the project or technical authority.
- **Small projects:** SA is required regardless of size; one person may fill several roles if the needed independence is kept, and SA may come from outside the project in the same organization (not preferred, but better than none); small acquirers may rely more on insight. Essential deliverables: classification record, SAP, requirements mapping matrix with tailoring, audit reports with corrective actions, safety analysis if safety-critical, and a risk register.
- **Software assurance:** perform SA, software safety and IV&V (if required) per NASA-STD-8739.8 and the project SAP (8739.8B task 1); SA product: SA process audit report with findings and corrective action plans (8.59); SAP must define metrics and roles of engineering, SMA and IV&V
- **Also:** Three tailoring levels: software classification (NPR 7150.2), the project's requirements mapping matrix, and SA, safety and IV&V adjustments by the SMA technical authority; tailoring needs rationale, risk evaluation, concurrence of affected organizations (safety, cybersecurity, quality), approval by the Center SMA TA, formal risk acceptance by the project manager, and notice to the next management level. If the class changes, update assurance plans and contracts. For acquired software SA is done by both acquirer and provider.

## SWE-023 Software Safety-Critical Requirements

%guidance[HB-023 interprets=SWE-023] If the project has safety-critical software, the project manager implements the safety-critical software requirements of NASA-STD-8739.8 in the project's software plans and software requirements specifications. The ETA and S&MA TA jointly determine safety-criticality, starting in Formulation (engineering and SA assess independently, compare and resolve differences; disagreements go up both TA chains), and reassess at each milestone and at lower levels (components, models, simulations) as the design matures. Software safety requirements cover must-work and must-not-work functions, both process (software hazard analysis tied to the system hazard analysis) and technical (SWE-134 items a to l: safe state, memory modification detection, input and output checks, command prerequisites, no single event hazard initiation, timely off-nominal response, error handling).

- **Objective evidence:** documentation identifying software as safety-critical with signed rationale and SMA and project concurrence (SRB or SMA decision record, safety-critical classification checklist, traceability of components to hazards); signed Software Safety Plan with SMA and TA approval, naming the 8739.8 paragraphs addressed, tailored processes, roles and hazard and V&V strategies; hazard tracking reports with risk levels, mitigations and status at PDR, CDR, TRR; signed design documents and architecture showing fault tolerance, isolation and defensive programming; code review and analysis records; safety test plans, results, fault injection results, signed verification closeout reports and independent validation reports; signed SMA concurrence at milestones and minutes; CM logs, change impact analyses and baselines for safety-critical components; safety training records
- **Documents:** software safety plan (5.18 per SWE-013); software requirements specification; Software Design Document; hazard reports and system hazard analysis; Safety Compliance Data Package (contracted software); software safety requirements mapping table
- **Milestones:** Determination in Formulation and reassessed at each milestone (PDR, CDR); software safety design analysis complete by Phase 2 safety review; test plan off-nominal analysis between Phase 2 and Phase 3; Phase 3 confirms test results close hazard verifications; hazard status tracked at PDR, CDR, TRR.
- **Small projects:** Requirements must still be met but the depth of analysis scales with safety risk; small projects may use tools already validated for safety-critical development (or establish a set for future use), reuse an established safety plan, assign multi-functional roles and simplify by risk.
- **Software assurance:** confirm identified safety-critical software components and data have implemented the safety-critical SA requirements of the standard (8739.8B task 1); SWE-134 derived SA tasks 1 to 6: analyze requirements and design for items a to l; assess source code against a to l at each code inspection, test review, safety review and project review; confirm safety-critical loaded and uplinked data, rules and scripts are tested; analyze design for partitioning and isolation of safety-critical elements; participate in reviews of safety-critical products; ensure SWE-134 implementation is consistent with the system hazard analysis; SA product: software safety requirements mapping table
- **Also:** Criteria for safety-critical: causes or contributes to a system hazard, controls safety-critical functions or hardware, performs hazard mitigation, prevents undesired consequences, responds within time-critical windows, detects and alerts on hazardous states (8739.8 Appendix A; HDBK-2203 Software Safety-Critical Assessment Tool). Basic steps: identify safety-critical software, document it, stop if none, determine criticality, determine effort and oversight. Tools used to build safety-critical software (compilers, generators, simulators) need validation or revalidation; OTS and reused software needs safety impact analysis; contracts specify safety deliverables (safety plan, hazard analyses, audits, verification reports) and the Safety Compliance Data Package responsibility. Programmable logic: NASA-HDBK-8739.23. Training through STEP; NASA-GB-8719.13 Appendix H generic safety checklist.

## SWE-024 Plan Tracking

%guidance[HB-024 interprets=SWE-024] The project manager tracks actual results and performance of software activities against the software plans, takes, records and manages corrective actions to closure, and takes, records and manages changes to commitments agreed by the affected groups. The software lead periodically evaluates cost, schedule, risk, technical performance and content (CMMI project monitoring and control) using charts, schedules, reports, integration meetings, test observations and team meetings. Discrepancies are tracked informally until a product baseline, then formally (usually PRACA) at the threshold set in the SDP/SMP or SCMP; changed plans require recommitment by the software lead and stakeholders through CM change control, signed memos of understanding or organizational policy.

- **Objective evidence:** progress reports (status, schedule updates, KPIs); milestone review records and minutes (SRR, PDR, CDR); schedule management records and logs of delayed tasks; execution metrics (planned versus actual test coverage, resources, defect trends); corrective action tracking logs (ID, description, action and rationale, owner, timeline, status, closure evidence); evidence of corrective action implementation; signed corrective action closure records; root cause analysis reports; change management logs (ID, description, justification, stakeholder approval signatures, status); change impact assessments; updated and approved plans; stakeholder communication records; issue and corrective action trend reports; progress performance logs; SA audit records and OSMA audit findings; training records for tracking and change control
- **Documents:** 5.08 SDP-SMP; 5.06 SCMP; 5.10 STP; IV&V plan; 5.01 CR-PR Software Change Request and Problem Report; PRACA system; 7.18 Documentation Guidance
- **Milestones:** Plans typically baselined at the phases shown in 7.08; tracking results feed stakeholder reviews (SWE-018); SA deliverables provided at regular intervals such as milestone reviews.
- **Small projects:** Applies regardless of size; engineering staff may perform some or all assurance duties; small projects can use a work tracking or CM tool (or a wiki) that notifies the team of document updates and re-baselining, with lightweight tracking of corrective actions and commitment changes.
- **Software assurance:** assess plans for compliance with NPR 7150.2 and NASA-STD-8739.8, including changes to commitments (8739.8B task 1); confirm closure of corrective actions for performance against plans, including closure rationale (task 2); confirm changes to commitments are recorded and managed (task 3); SA products: activity tracking reports, issue and corrective action reports with closure evidence, trend analysis reports, change impact assessments
- **Also:** Corrective action closeout record should hold: issue description, proposed action with acceptable limits, actual results and impacts, required changes to requirements, schedules and resources, and signatures or concurrence of relevant stakeholders; then stored in a repository or CM system. SA guards against premature closure without evidence of implementation. The NPR mandates no particular tool or approach as long as these elements are used.

## SWE-027 Use of Commercial, Government, and Legacy Software

%guidance[HB-027 interprets=SWE-027] For any COTS, GOTS, MOTS, OSS or reused component the project manager ensures (a) the requirements it must meet are identified, (b) it has documentation adequate for its purpose, (c) proprietary, usage, ownership, warranty, licensing and transfer rights and conditions of use (notices, copyleft such as GPL v3) are addressed with Center IP Counsel, (d) future support is planned and adequate, (e) it is verified and validated to the level required of a similar developed component, and (f) vendor-reported defects are periodically assessed. Per the NPR note, the procuring project documents before procurement a plan to V&V the software to the developed-software level, and these components (and auto-generated code) meet the Appendix C requirements for their class. Guidance adds: test only the functions used and disable or prove safe the rest; supplement missing documentation; consider source code escrow and supplier-loss contingencies; feed vendor defect reports into hazard evaluations; keep configuration-managed copies of board support packages and drivers and monitor vendor and user forums.

- **Objective evidence:** requirements for each COTS, GOTS, MOTS, OSS or reused component in the SRS, linked to project requirements; vendor or community documentation (user guides, API references, installation instructions, license terms, maintenance and support documentation); test plans, procedures and reports showing V&V for intended use to the developed-software standard; signed agreements or approvals from Center IP Counsel on rights, usage and warranties; logs of periodic vendor defect reviews with impact assessments, risks and mitigations
- **Documents:** Software Requirements Specification; software management plan or standalone OTS management plan; software maintenance, retirement, safety and assurance plans; data acceptance package and version description documents; 6.3 RTOS checklist; 6.4 OTS checklist; PAT-024; PAT-025
- **Milestones:** V&V plan documented before procurement (NPR note); OTS integration addressed at the system PDR and CDR (SA checklist).
- **Small projects:** Applies regardless of size; small projects take a risk-based, tailored approach, may lean on off-the-shelf software and automation, but must still plan long-term support for it.
- **Software assurance:** confirm conditions a through f are complete for any COTS, GOTS, MOTS, OSS or reused software acquired or used (8739.8B task 1); SA products: requirements assessment report, documentation review checklist, rights and licensing compliance report, support plan assessment report, V&V test report, vendor defect assessment plan and log; for safety-critical components confirm hazard contributions and controls are tracked and discrepancies and workarounds dispositioned with SA approval; confirm risk analyses, trade studies or heritage reviews were done
- **Also:** The Guidance tab has a long question checklist for assessing COTS, MOTS, OSS and reuse (pedigree, vendor stability, escrow, documentation, training, problem sharing, end-of-vendor plans, unused features, V&V approach, safety-critical use, OS traits, reliability data, version upgrades, licensing seats and transfer, compilers and autocoders, infrastructure tools); the SA tab has an example COTS safety checklist (trade studies, SMP coverage of upgrades and version control, class and SA concurrence, maintenance and retirement plans, requirements and unused functions, design review participation, hazard analysis approach, V&V, reliability, metrics, lessons learned). Outside certifications (for example FAA) are used with engineering judgment. Reused code needs an effort assessment and V&V of modifications.

## SWE-032 CMMI Levels for Class A and B Software

%guidance[HB-032 interprets=SWE-032] Class A software is acquired, developed and maintained only by an organization with a non-expired CMMI-DEV Maturity Level 3 (or higher) rating, and Class B (except on NASA Class D payloads per NPR 8705.4) by one with Level 2 or higher, appraised by a CMMI Institute Certified Lead Appraiser against CMMI-DEV V2.0, posted on the CMMI Institute site or disclosed in an Appraisal Disclosure Statement, and kept current for the whole development and maintenance period. It is an organizational qualifier: in-house teams, contractors and subcontractors all need the rating (flowed down in contracts), and the acquisition team must be supported by software engineering and SA representatives from a rated organization (Level 3 for Class A, Level 2 for Class B; extent set by the Center ETA) who help write supplier agreements and review software deliverables. For Class B a best-in-class provider without the rating may be used if a qualified evaluator picked by the Center ETA evaluates it against Level 2 practices, the project funds the evaluation and mitigates deficiencies, and the OCE and Center ETA are notified with the results.

- **Objective evidence:** valid CMMI-DEV certificate from a Certified Lead Appraiser (Level 3 Class A, Level 2 Class B), not expired (typically 3 years); scope of certification (organizational unit, locations, software processes); appraisal method reference (SCAMPI A); final appraisal findings report excerpt with process areas, scope and dates overlapping the contract; Lead Appraiser correspondence; supplier evaluation records and checklists; supplier selection rationale; NASA audit or review results; compliance checklist from a milestone review (SRR, TRR); signed software classification worksheet and concurrence; re-certification documents and periodic compliance audit reports; signed approvals of exceptions (ETA or SEPG)
- **Documents:** supplier agreements and contracts (7.03); software classification record; Class B exception evaluation records (kept by the Center ETA for the life of the project); waiver as a streamlined compliance matrix
- **Milestones:** Confirm rating validity at major life cycle reviews via the CMMI Institute published appraisals site; compliance checklist at SRR or TRR given as an evidence example.
- **Small projects:** Small projects use Agency, Center and organizational assets and tailor lightweight CMMI practices to their scale while keeping the intent (the page cites a 2010 NDIA study on CMMI in small businesses).
- **Software assurance:** confirm Class A and B software is acquired, developed and maintained by an organization with a non-expired CMMI-DEV rating (8739.8B task 1); assess process-related issues, findings or risks from the CMMI assessment (task 2); perform audits on the software development and SA processes (task 3); SA products: assessment of CMMI findings, SA process audit report, development process audit report, process risk identification, confirmation of rating validity
- **Also:** Supplier agreements state technical requirements, deliverables, access to work products, NPR 7150.2 compliance and exceptions, status reporting, acceptance criteria, and non-technical terms (licensing, ownership, OSS, maintenance); software engineering and SA evaluate deliverables for acceptance criteria, completeness and accuracy. Center-wide exclusions or alternate requirements go through a waiver (streamlined compliance matrix) approved by the Engineering and SMA TAs. The guidance still mentions Capability Level and CMMI V1.3 process areas for Class B, which the Rev C and D text dropped (see Report).

## SWE-033 Acquisition vs. Development Assessment

%guidance[HB-033 interprets=SWE-033] The project manager assesses options for acquiring versus developing software, weighing risk, cost and benefit for each (NPR note options: buy off-the-shelf, develop internally, develop by contract, enhance existing, reuse existing, use source code available outside NASA) through trade studies, internal assessments and cost-benefit analyses. The team identifies technical, supplier, cost and schedule risks, documents the analysis and the raw data behind the decision, and involves technical staff, management, contracts, procurement, end users, customers and the Technical Authority; software used for human space flight components needs rigorous risk mitigation whatever its class. If off-the-shelf software is chosen, a V&V plan to the developed-software level is documented before procurement (SWE-027).

## SWE-052 Bidirectional Traceability

%guidance[HB-052 interprets=SWE-052] The project manager performs, records and maintains two-way traces between software elements per the class table: higher-level to software requirements (A, B, C, F), software requirements to system hazards (A, B, C, D), requirements to design (A, B, C), design to code (A, B, C), requirements to verifications (A, B, C, D, F) and requirements to non-conformances (A, B, C, D, F). Hazard traces cover hazard controls, mitigations, conditions and events and identify safety-critical components.

- **Objective evidence:** populated RTM linking system requirements, software requirements, design, code, tests and hazards with unique IDs (tool export or table); design review artifacts showing traceability assessed at SRR, SDR, PDR, CDR (checklists, findings, coverage charts, signed minutes); test plans, procedures and reports mapped to requirements, test case trace reports and execution results; version-controlled RTM and change logs, baseline traceability audit reports; hazard analysis reports traced to software requirements and mitigation tests; lessons learned and traceability gap analysis and corrective action reports
- **Documents:** 5.09 SRS; 5.13 SwDD; traceability matrix (electronic, in a requirements tool or spreadsheet); test procedures; hazard analysis reports (HAR), fault trees, FMEA; configuration management system
- **Milestones:** matrix reviewed at major phases and key reviews; review evidence cited at SRR, SDR, PDR and CDR; requirements verified over time tracked at SRR, SDR, TRR and post-deployment.
- **Small projects:** Spreadsheets, simple databases or Word documents with unique IDs can serve as the RTM (requirement ID, description, priority, linked design, code, tests, verification status), kept current by a named owner with periodic reviews and version control. Value-based tracing (full trace for high-priority and safety-critical requirements, lighter for others) is offered, but may not be acceptable where full traceability is mandated; safety-critical requirements always get full traceability.
- **Software assurance:** confirm bidirectional traceability has been completed, recorded and maintained (8739.8B task 1); confirm traceability includes any hazard that includes software (task 2); SA products: analysis of bidirectional traceability (completeness, gaps, rationale), hazard analysis traceability with corrective actions before milestones, traceability results with matrices, trends and corrective actions, integration with HARs, fault trees and FMEA
- **Also:** Matrix practices: create at project start; number requirements to show hierarchy; record source document or parent ID; list each requirement once; consider including requirement text; no empty cells in the requirements-to-tests matrix; trace unit tests to code and design, integration tests to high-level design, system tests to the SRS; identify tests for safety-critical functions. History: SWE-059, SWE-064 and SWE-072 were merged into this SWE in rev C. SA guidance 7.4.1 repeats 8739.8 safety-critical items, including 100% code test coverage for safety-critical components (or a risk assessment) and cyclomatic complexity of 10 or lower (or a risk assessment).

## SWE-053 Manage Requirements Changes

%guidance[HB-053 interprets=SWE-053] The project plans for and runs a defined, structured process for how a requirement change is submitted, evaluated, decided and incorporated into the baseline, normally through a Change Control Board and configuration or requirements management tools that record what changed, who, when and why. Each change gets an impact analysis (other software and system parts, safety, reliability, performance, rework, cost, schedule, technical) using traceability, and the results and decisions are recorded and communicated to stakeholders. Approved changes flow into plans, affected work products, traceability, verification and validation, and the data dictionary or ICD content; for Class A, changes affecting safety-critical or hazardous functionality get particular analysis.

- **Objective evidence:** change request forms with original requirement, proposed change, rationale and impact analysis; CCB minutes with approval or rejection; change request log (CR ID, submission date, status, decision rationale); updated SRS with version history and change log; updated RTM; impact analysis reports and updated hazard analyses; interdisciplinary coordination records; configuration management logs and closure evidence for affected items; updated test plans, regression plans, test results and validation summary; risk register entries, updated FMEA or fault trees, risk closure reports; requirements volatility, change status and non-conformance metrics; SA audit findings, independent review reports, lessons learned
- **Documents:** 5.01 CR-PR (minimum change request data); 5.09 SRS; 5.07 SDD; ICD; configuration management system; traceability matrix
- **Milestones:** TBD resolution tracked against PDR and CDR (example in evidence tab).
- **Small projects:** Track changes in a requirements log spreadsheet (requirement ID, original requirement, proposed change, reason, approval status, date modified, related tests or artifacts) with a lightweight request-for-change, small review and approval group, update and notify steps. Hold brief reviews in team meetings, keep a simple trace table, give safety-critical changes priority and a safety impact review, archive old SRS versions, and audit informally.
- **Software assurance:** confirm requirements changes are documented, tracked, approved and maintained throughout the life cycle (8739.8B task 1); SA products: volatility analysis and risk assessment, change management system audit and compliance report, defect analysis of defects introduced by requirements changes with mitigation recommendations
- **Also:** The metrics tab states three metrics are required for all projects under NASA SA policy: requirements volatility, change status trend over time, open vs. closed non-conformances over time. Example volatility rate: changes (added plus deleted plus modified) divided by baseline requirement count, times 100. SA guidance recommends an impact analysis checklist covering safety, cost, schedule and risk, and a documented safety impact assessment for every safety-critical requirement change. Other tasks: update plans, raise CRs for affected work products, verify and validate changed requirements, update traceability, ensure only latest documents are used.

## SWE-054 Corrective Action for Inconsistencies

%guidance[HB-054 interprets=SWE-054] The project follows a documented corrective action process (usually in the SDP/SMP or the Software Assurance Plan) to find inconsistencies among requirements, plans and software products, record them and their source, initiate formally documented corrective actions, and track them to closure. Consistency is checked through periodic reviews and as part of change control (noting where changed requirements flow into plans and products), with bidirectional traceability as the main tool. The process provides a tracking system (PRACA or CR/PR tool), a review board such as the CCB, an implementation step including test procedure updates and regression tests, a resolution time frame, an escalation path to the PM, and archiving.

- **Objective evidence:** inconsistency tracking log (ID, affected artifacts, description, risk severity and priority, assignee, status); review and CCB minutes with issues, owners and target dates; root cause analysis reports for major or recurring inconsistencies; corrective action tracker (action ID, description, priority, owner, steps, status) cross-referenced to inconsistencies; closure validation reports; change request forms, CCB minutes and updated artifacts with version history; consistency review reports from PDR, CDR, TRR and completed review checklists; updated RTM and its version log; test reports, regression evidence and defect closure records; corrective action and consistency metrics
- **Documents:** 5.08 SDP-SMP or 8.51 Software Assurance Plan (corrective action process); 5.01 CR-PR; corrective action database or PRACA system; RTM
- **Milestones:** consistency checks at milestone reviews such as PDR, CDR and TRR; all inconsistencies resolved before delivery or closure (small projects).
- **Small projects:** Use an "Inconsistency Tracker" spreadsheet (ID, affected artifacts, description, corrective action, responsible person, status, date closed) or a free issue tracker, reconcile requirements, plans and products in regular team meetings and milestone reviews, and give one person responsibility. Prioritize safety-critical, interface and milestone-blocking issues and close each only after verifying the artifacts agree.
- **Software assurance:** monitor differences among requirements, plans and software products and confirm they are addressed and corrective actions tracked to closure (8739.8B task 1); SA products: list of inconsistencies (artifact, description, risk, suggested action, owner), change management system results, problem reporting or defect tracking results
- **Also:** SA metrics list is headed "Required Metrics". SA evaluation checklist for proposed changes: processed through CCB; safety evaluation for safety-impacting updates; categorized as error correction or new requirement; downstream artifacts updated; regression testing shows no new problems. In safety vs. security conflicts, safety takes precedence. Warning signs of inconsistency: unstable or unclear requirements, plans made before requirements stabilize, budget changes, poor communication, weak change control, inexperienced staff, turnover.

## SWE-055 Requirements Validation

%guidance[HB-055 interprets=SWE-055] The project validates, with the customer and stakeholders, that requirements are feasible, necessary, sufficient and reflect their intent, so the software does the right thing in the customer's target environment ("build the right product", as opposed to verification). Validation is planned, not ad hoc: the plan names the products to validate, acceptance criteria and techniques, and lives in a V&V plan, the STP or the SDP/SMP. It runs through the life cycle: stakeholder review of the SRS, review of the SwDD and ICDs/IDDs, acceptance test procedures and reports (SA witnesses validation testing for safety-critical software), and release with user manuals and training material.

- **Objective evidence:** requirements validation plan (methods, tools, schedule, personnel; strategy for safety-critical, human-rated and mission-critical requirements); requirements review records and minutes; stakeholder approvals and sign-offs; documented validation criteria; RTM tracing requirements to design, test cases and expected-environment requirements; traceability gap reports; test procedures, test cases and test reports; simulation results; IV&V checklists and independent validation reports (if applicable); risk logs and discrepancy resolution logs; validation coverage, requirements volatility and risk trend metrics
- **Documents:** 5.09 SRS; 5.13 SwDD; 5.02 IDD; ICDs; 5.10 STP or V&V plan or SVVP; 5.08 SDP-SMP; 5.14 Test Procedures (acceptance); 5.11 STR (acceptance test report); 5.12 SUM; RTM; 5.17 SA Plan (SA validation plans and methods)
- **Milestones:** SRR should address "getting the right requirements" and "getting the requirements right" (7.09); SA metrics reviewed at milestone reviews such as PDR, CDR, TRR
- **Small projects:** Rigor may scale with class (D/E can share roles and combine verification with validation, for example during code reviews or integration tests), but safety-critical, human-rated and mission-critical requirements still need robust validation, with the methods recorded in the SDP/SMP. Even small projects document steps, tools, results and stakeholder sign-off, and may use risk-based prioritization, incremental validation, informal stakeholder buy-in and a simple traceability spreadsheet.
- **Software assurance:** confirm that project testing has shown the software will function as expected in the customer environment (NASA-STD-8739.8B task 1); SA products include requirements analysis (8.54), design analysis (8.55) and testing analysis (8.57), RTM review, test procedure and report review, and test witnessing signatures (SWE-066)
- **Also:** Validation defined (NPR 7150.2): confirmation through objective evidence that requirements for a specific intended use are fulfilled. Basic process: select validation criterion for a product, examine for correctness, completeness and compliance, record findings, repeat until all criteria are covered. Products to validate by phase: SRS, interface and system requirements; SwDD, IDD, ICDs; acceptance test procedures and reports; SUM. Example acceptance criteria: test inputs and outputs, expected and acceptable results, service levels, customer expectations. Techniques: formal reviews, peer reviews/inspections, prototypes and demonstrations, analysis, beta testing, paper prototyping, modeling and simulation (minimized, since the aim is the real environment), use cases, viewpoint-oriented validation, formal methods, test case reviews; security techniques: M&S, penetration testing, ethical hacking, fuzz testing, replay testing. Suggested reviewer roles: customer, developer, tester, systems engineer, hardware engineer, fault management engineer, SA and safety, SRS author, SMEs. RTM may hold links to parent requirements, design, code, tests, validation method and result reference. Re-validate requirements as they change.

## SWE-057 Software Architecture

%guidance[HB-057 interprets=SWE-057] The project transforms the software requirements (allocated and derived) into a recorded architecture: the software structure, its components, the interfaces among them and to external software and hardware, and the quality attributes (performance, modifiability, security and others) it must achieve. The architecture is drafted during requirements and operations concept work, refined throughout development, and baselined at PDR. It is recorded in the Software Design Description (SwDD), with driving requirements, stakeholders and concerns, assumptions and constraints, alternatives assessed and rationale, decomposition and dependencies, verification methods, and risks.

- **Objective evidence:** SRS and derived requirements traced to system requirements; stakeholder and interface requirements; software architecture description (structure, components, quality attributes, patterns); subsystem decomposition; interface design documents and ICDs; requirements-to-architecture traceability matrix (including non-functional requirements); risk analysis and alternatives analysis reports; review board findings; UML models, data flow diagrams, execution and thread architecture; architecture validation results (simulation, prototypes); SARB reports and review minutes; safety hazard analyses and mission assurance reports for architecture features (partitioning, redundancy); SwDD architecture sections; baselined architecture under CM and change request records; architecture metrics reports
- **Documents:** 5.13 SwDD (required content includes the architectural design); 7.07 Software Architecture Description (recommended content and example outlines); ICDs; IDDs
- **Milestones:** architecture drafted during requirements scoping and ConOps work; baselined at PDR (see 7.08)
- **Small projects:** Smaller or low-risk projects may use lightweight, risk-driven architecture (block diagrams, flowcharts, simplified UML), but the architecture must still be documented adequately. At minimum: system overview of major components, interfaces, constraints and assumptions, risk mitigation decisions, and changes with rationale.
- **Software assurance:** assess that the architecture addresses software structure, qualities, interfaces and external/internal components (8739.8B task 1); analyze the architecture to assess whether software safety and mission assurance requirements are met (task 2); products: software design analysis results on the architecture, SA risk analysis, safety and mission assurance impact list (partitioning, redundancy, interface handling, resource management), SARB findings where used, summary of non-conformances and resolutions
- **Also:** NPR note defines content: structure, quality attributes, known interfaces between components and to external software and hardware, interfaces among components, and the components; it cites the SARB paper "Quality Attributes for Mission Flight Software: A Reference for Architects" (NTRS 20160005787). Architecture vs. detailed design: architecture is high-level structure and abstractions with allocated requirements traced to elements; detailed design gives data types, signatures and units traceable to code. Domain-specific content to record: patterns, data and resource management, real-time execution attributes, dependency structure models. Guidance recommends periodic architecture reviews (for example a Software Architecture Review Board).

## SWE-058 Detailed Design

%guidance[HB-058 interprets=SWE-058] The project develops, records and maintains a software design that elaborates the architecture down to lower-level units (modules, classes, functions, interfaces, data structures) detailed enough to be coded, compiled and tested. A preliminary design is addressed at PDR and the design is baselined at CDR; it is kept current as requirements change, with design changes under configuration control and test plans updated to match. Before detailed design the team checks readiness: documented architecture, SRS, SDP/SMP, coding standards, trained staff, reuse and COTS analysis, alternatives analysis.

- **Objective evidence:** SwDD (lower-level components, interfaces, data design, performance details); IDD; requirements-to-design and design-to-code traceability matrices; design peer review records and resolutions; PDR artifacts; CDR artifacts (design approval, closure of action items, maturity assessment); SDP/SMP showing the architecture-to-design process; design baselines and change logs under CM; safety requirements implementation records linked to hazard analyses; fault tolerance evidence; hazard tracking and closure logs; coding-readiness evidence (decomposition to units, pseudocode, flowcharts or UML, algorithm descriptions); alignment with coding standards; design risk logs and closure; lessons learned incorporation; SA design analysis; metrics reports
- **Documents:** 5.13 SwDD; 5.02 IDD; 5.07 SDD (Software Data Dictionary); 5.08 SDP-SMP; 5.06 SCMP; 5.09 SRS; 5.12 SUM
- **Milestones:** preliminary design validated at PDR; design baselined at CDR; teams review PDR success criteria (7.09) before starting detailed design
- **Small projects:** Small projects should reuse Center PAL coding standards, SwDD/IDD/data dictionary templates, accredited tools and heritage components rather than inventing their own, and may use a minimal viable design (modules, interfaces, logic flows and data structures adequate for coding and testing) refined iteratively. The page gives a short before and during design checklist (standards and templates approved, reuse identified, traceability maintained).
- **Software assurance:** assess the design against hardware and software requirements and identify gaps (8739.8B task 1); assess that the design is consistent with the architecture and describes lower-level units to be coded, compiled and tested (task 2); assess that the design does not introduce undesirable behaviors or unnecessary capabilities (task 3); confirm the design implements all required safety-critical functions and requirements (task 4); perform an SA design analysis (task 5, see 8.55); products: design analysis results, design risks and issues list, safety-critical design implementation assessment against hazard analyses (SWE-205), design coverage traceability matrix, peer review feedback
- **Also:** Checklists named: PAT-006 Design Practices for Safety; PAT-008 Safety Considerations for Design Peer Reviews; PAT-014 Architecture Design Checklist; PAT-015 Detailed Design Checklist; PAT-016 Functional Design Checklist; PAT-020 Examples of Interface Problems; PAT-021 SADESIGN Checklist (SA design analysis); PAT-031 Critical Design Analysis Checklist; PAT-036 Software Architecture and Design Process Audit; PAT-047 Architecture and Detailed Design Assessment; PAT-048 IDD Assessment; PAT-055 Software Data Dictionary Assessment. During design: CASE tools accredited (SWE-136), all requirements traceable to design and every design element to a requirement, all work products identified, data element characteristics (type, format, size, units) recorded in the data dictionary. Data design covers structures, formats, types, units and ranges. Secure coding practices (SWE-207, SWE-185) are to be built in. Rationale for major design decisions should be recorded.

## SWE-060 Coding Software

%guidance[HB-060 interprets=SWE-060] After architecture and detailed design, the project turns the design into executable code (algorithms, data structures, interfaces) that faithfully implements it. Guidance emphasizes following the project coding standard (SWE-061, also binding on suppliers), using accredited tools and verified auto-generated code (SWE-136, 8.11), unit testing, static analysis and peer review during coding, and balancing optimization against maintainability. It includes a checklist of safe C programming practices.

- **Objective evidence:** bidirectional traceability matrix design to code (no orphan code or orphan design); traceability tool reports (requirements to design to code to tests); version control logs with review approvals per commit; design-to-code transition checklist; code comments referencing design artifacts; static analysis reports; code coverage reports; coding standards compliance report; unit test reports; test procedures traced to design; dynamic analysis results (stack, memory, timing); CM records linking code to design baselines; change impact analyses; build logs; code review records and peer review checklists; implementation audit reports; design inspection records; test matrices; prototypes; model-to-code comparison reports; implementation plan; developer logbook; safety-critical code analysis; implementation progress, defect density and improvement metrics
- **Small projects:** Small projects can use lightweight design documentation, iterative or MVP implementation, simple IDEs and accredited tools, and lightweight static analyzers and unit test frameworks. A named person or the project manager should oversee that the design is faithfully implemented, with frequent check-ins and code walkthroughs.
- **Software assurance:** confirm the code implements the software designs (8739.8B task 1); confirm the code has no functionality not defined in the design or requirements (task 2); products: design analysis results with traceability, code quality analysis (standards, secure coding, dead code), requirements analysis results (gaps either way), static code analysis results, code coverage data
- **Also:** Safe C practices listed: limit parameter count and size and pass large structures by reference; bounded recursion; custom boundary checks; replace unsafe functions such as gets; memmove for overlapping memory and wrappers with error handling; switch with default and break instead of deep if chains; initialize all variables; validate pointers and avoid function pointers; prototypes; no signed/unsigned mixing and care with floating-point comparison; treat warnings as errors; reentrant library calls in multitasking; avoid ?: and use named constants; no compiler-specific assumptions. SA 7.4.1 has a programming practices checklist for safety and reliability. PAT-032 covers interrupts.

## SWE-061 Coding Standards

%guidance[HB-061 interprets=SWE-061] At project start the project selects (preferably an existing standard such as MISRA C or CERT C, tailored only as needed), defines and documents its coding methods, standards and criteria, then adheres to them for the whole life cycle, including in supplier contracts. Adherence is verified by automated static analysis (nightly or in CI, plus compiler warnings), peer reviews and inspections with checklists, and the team is trained on the standard. Human-rated and mission-critical software should apply a recognized coding standard supported by automated analysis tools, and secure coding standards where security matters.

- **Objective evidence:** coding standards document (formatting, error handling, secure coding, restricted constructs, language specifics, tailoring guidance); SDP section on enforcement, roles and verification tools; configuration management plan; coding standard training records; tailoring records with justification, risk analysis and approvals; static analysis reports with severity and trends; code review records with checklists and resolutions; SA verification reports; CI or nightly build logs; samples of compliant code; deviation logs (approved exceptions, unauthorized deviations with root cause and correction); test cases and traceability; compliance, quality and security metrics; lessons learned evidence; SA risk reports
- **Documents:** coding standards document; 5.08 SDP-SMP (by name, not number; coding standards identified and enforcement described); configuration management plan; supplier contracts (see 7.03)
- **Small projects:** Adopt an existing standard (MISRA C, CERT C, PEP 8) instead of writing one, or publish a one or two page standard with a small set of essential rules (naming, module size, globals and memory, error handling, testability). Enforce it with free static analyzers in CI and light peer reviews with a short checklist.
- **Software assurance:** assure the project manager selected and/or defined coding methods, standards and criteria (8739.8B task 1); analyze that the code conforms to all required coding methods, rules and principles (task 2); products: SA static analysis of source against the standard (including secure coding such as CERT C, CWE Top 25), independent evaluation of code and of the developers' static analysis coverage, coding standard document verification, SA risk reports; 7.4 adds review of the standards in the SDP (SWE-013) and independent static analysis with a defined issue review and closure process
- **Also:** Standard vs. style: standards govern correctness, safety, reliability and security; style governs readability. A standard should cover code structure and module size limits, error and exception handling, library use, global data restrictions, safe language subsets, and format, naming and comments; rules may carry severity levels (mandatory, advisory with justified deviation, permissive). Static analyzer conformance to ISO/IEC TS 17961 is recommended for secure C. Developers close out all tool and compiler warnings before formal code review. Interpret "methods" and "criteria" as the developer's style. Language checklists: 6.5 C, 6.6 C++, 6.7 Ada, 6.8 Fortran, 6.9 generic, 6.10 general good practices, 6.11 exception handling; PAT-022 Programming Practices Checklist.

## SWE-062 Unit Test

%guidance[HB-062 interprets=SWE-062] The developer unit tests each unit (a separately testable element of the design) with full insight into the code, including off-nominal, error and robustness cases, against success criteria set before execution and on an approved schedule per the test plan. Results must be repeatable, captured systematically, evaluated where feasible by someone other than the tester, with issues documented and corrected and all test artifacts kept for regression. Safety-critical units need MC/DC coverage; SA or IV&V (or, less formally, a designated team member such as the lead) verifies that unit tests are complete and properly executed; under CI/CD all unit tests rerun on every code update.

- **Objective evidence:** approved software test plan with unit test objectives, criteria, tools and schedule; unit test case descriptions (inputs, expected outputs, environment, pass/fail criteria); test environment configuration and known differences from target; configuration snapshots of the software under test; unit test logs (case ID, date, tester, inputs, outputs, status, repeatability); automated test reports; SA witness logs for critical tests (optional, high-risk projects); code coverage reports (statement, decision, MC/DC for safety-critical); requirements-to-test traceability matrix; problem reports with severity, root cause, fix and verification; defect closure before integration; regression test results; independent evaluations of results and peer review logs; safety-critical unit test records; unit test report (tests run, outcomes, metrics, defects, lessons); captured test data, scripts, stubs, drivers, procedures and deviations; SDF or engineering notebook entries; unit test metrics reports.
- **Documents:** 5.10 STP; Software Development Folders (SDFs) or equivalent repository; 5.08 SDP-SMP; 5.06 SCMP; problem reports; unit test report.
- **Small projects:** Small projects may use leaner, automated, risk-focused unit testing, but their test plans must still describe the environment and its gaps from operations and record inputs, expected versus actual outputs and pass/fail. They may use Center lean procedures and lightweight tools, let developers test their own code with peer or lightweight independent verification, and must still record environment, results, defects and procedure compliance.
- **Software assurance:** confirm the project executes the required unit tests, especially those for safety-critical functions; confirm errors, defects and problem reports found in unit testing are tracked to closure (NASA-STD-8739.8B, no task IDs given).
- **Also:** Unit defined per IEEE Std 610.12-1990 (separately testable design element, logically separable program part, or component not subdivided further). Minor issues such as typos may be fixed without documentation if project protocols allow. Automated unit tests are preferred so they join the regression suite. Unit test documentation is evidence for safety-critical software reviews (NASA-GB-8719.13). SA products (7.2): unit test results, problem or defect reports, test validation records.

## SWE-063 Release Version Description

%guidance[HB-063 interprets=SWE-063] Every software release, and sub-release, gets a Software Version Description (SVD, also called VDD) that is the authoritative record of what the release contains, what changed, how to install it, and how it relates to prior versions and baselines. Each release has a unique version identifier under configuration management that tracks source, dependencies and build configuration, supports rollback, and archives prior SVDs; each new SVD documents all changes from the prior version so readers need no other document. The SVD should reflect code that passed all planned verification and validation, and flag deferred tests or partial compliance.

- **Objective evidence:** completed and signed-off SVD/VDD for each release; configuration management records (release tags or branches, change history, archived builds); release package (executables, source, build scripts, dependency manifests); validation and test reports for the release; Physical Configuration Audit (PCA) records and signed checklists; traceability matrices (requirements planned vs. implemented, defects to release, change requests); security assurance artifacts (virus scan results, vulnerability scans, resolved and open cybersecurity issues); metrics and trend reports; issue tracking records of non-conformances; build logs and configuration or dependency validation artifacts.
- **Documents:** 5.16 VDD; 7.18 Documentation Guidance (SVD content); configuration management records; PCA records; issue tracking system.
- **Small projects:** A small project's SVD can cover just release ID, date and purpose, a change summary, configuration details (environment, dependencies, repository tag), installation steps, lightweight traceability to requirements and defect IDs, and known issues. Keep it simple and automated (templates, Git tags, CI pipelines, release-note generators), build it incrementally as a living document, keep it in the repository, and make one person responsible for each release's SVD.
- **Software assurance:** confirm the project creates a correct SVD for each release; for each release, confirm the software was scanned for security defects and coding standard compliance and confirm the results (NASA-STD-8739.8B, no task IDs given).
- **Also:** SVD contents: release inventory with version IDs of every component (libraries, APIs, third-party tools); functional changes, bug fixes with defect report references, known limitations and workarounds; installation and deployment instructions; dependencies and traceability to prior versions and baselines; references to supporting artifacts and V&V results. Version scheme should be unique and support major, minor and build levels. SA tracking entries for non-conformances record description and impact, phase found, assignee and timeline, and status. SA guidance (7.4): confirm the software is virus free (scan with current antivirus, record tool and date); a PCA is held for releases delivered to external groups (another Center, contractor, operational customer), with SA conducting or participating; SA products: non-conformance list in the tracking system and verified SVD data per release.

## SWE-065 Test Plan, Procedures, Reports

%guidance[HB-065 interprets=SWE-065] The project establishes and keeps current four things: software test plans (scope, strategy, levels, roles, tools and environments, risks, resources and schedule), test procedures (step-by-step cases with inputs, expected outputs and pass/fail criteria, covering requirements, interfaces, boundaries, errors, stress, fault recovery and hazard mitigations, dry-run before formal execution), the tests themselves including any code written to run them, and test reports (cases run, pass/fail, deviations, analysis, anomalies with root cause, traceability to requirements). For multi-build software, plans include phased validation with final end-to-end testing; reused or legacy software is retested for modified, critical and historically risky components. Test documents are updated through formal review when design, requirements, tools, coverage findings, classification or safety-criticality change.

- **Objective evidence:** version-controlled test plan with traceability to requirements and safety rationale (for example baselined at CDR with tracked updates); test plan approval records (project manager or lead, SA/QA, independent reviewers when required); peer review reports or minutes with attendees, anomalies and actions; test plan content coverage analysis; test procedure documents; traceability matrix from tests to requirements and hazard controls; dry-run records; configuration management records of procedure updates; SA/QA sign-off of revised procedures; timestamped test execution logs (case ID, inputs, outputs, anomalies, environment); automation logs; test monitoring and witness checklists; software test reports (outcomes, discrepancies, root causes, coverage analysis); test effectiveness metrics; SA/QA sign-off of test reports; hazard traceability matrices; off-nominal, fault-injection, stress and boundary test logs; witness checklists for hazard tests; change logs for test documents (what, why, who approved, when); revised test artifacts and regression re-run records; metrics charts; test repository and tool exports.
- **Documents:** 5.10 STP; 5.14 Software Test Procedures; 5.11 STR; 7.18 Documentation Guidance; configuration management system for test code.
- **Milestones:** SA confirms at PDR that the test plan has been started with placeholders for safety-critical scenarios, and at CDR that test procedures have been started and address operational, off-nominal and boundary conditions; the objective evidence example shows the test plan baselined at CDR.
- **Small projects:** Small projects may combine the test plan, procedures and results into one document, using procedures with embedded result fields (test ID, requirement verified, description, expected and actual result, pass/fail, notes). They can rely on standardized lightweight templates and checklists, risk-based testing, reuse of legacy tests, automation, and peer or external review of critical tests when full tester independence is not possible, while keeping documents under configuration management and periodically reviewed.
- **Software assurance:** part a: confirm test plans exist, have correct content and are maintained; confirm the plan covers verification of safety-critical software, specifically off-nominal scenarios; part b: confirm test procedures exist and are updated when tests or requirements change; analyze procedures for requirements coverage, pass/fail criteria, operational and off-nominal including boundary conditions, requirements and hazard coverage per SWE-066 and SWE-192, and cybersecurity coverage per SWE-157 and SWE-210; part c: confirm test code is kept in a configuration management system, its issues are recorded, and its defects are tracked to closure; part d: confirm test reports are created and maintained through integration and test, contain as-run data, results and required approvals, all issues found in each test are recorded, and errors and defects are tracked to closure (NASA-STD-8739.8B, no task IDs given).
- **Also:** Formal and qualification testing runs on hardware closely matching the target (processor, memory, timing, interfaces, data rates) or a high-fidelity simulation (SWE-073). All procedures get a dry run before formal witnessed testing. Regression testing follows every modification (SWE-191). SA products (7.2): test plan review with evidence of SA approval, procedure reviews, test status assessments, approval of test reports where required (for example safety-critical), issue tracking and root cause, safety test validation (FDIR, concurrent failures), categorized issue lists.

## SWE-066 Perform Testing

%guidance[HB-066 interprets=SWE-066] The project tests the software against all its documented functional, performance, safety-critical and interface requirements across unit, integration, system, regression and special (stress, stability, safety) testing, with every test traceable to requirements. Qualification or formal testing is done by people independent of the detailed design and implementation; for multi-build software, qualification waits for the item's final build. Code coverage analysis finds unexecuted and orphaned paths; defects are reported with severity, root cause and corrective action and failed tests are rerun after fixes; regression testing follows every change.

- **Objective evidence:** software test plan; test traceability matrix with forward and backward traces (SWE-052); defined entry and exit criteria for test phases; test readiness checklist results (PAT-026, PAT-027); approved test procedures covering nominal, off-nominal, stress and recovery; annotated test data sets; testing constraints documentation; test execution logs (date, time, configuration, personnel, inputs, outputs, status); SA witnessing signatures; dynamic test results; code coverage reports; regression test results; discrepancy or non-conformance logs with root cause and retest; safety-critical test results; hazard reports addressed through tests with SA sign-off on hazard traceability (SWE-192); off-nominal and stress results; test reports; metrics reports; updated traceability matrices; non-conformance reports by severity; defect tracking and resolution logs; change management logs (SWE-080); simulation environment validation reports; TRR minutes and approvals; FQT results; AI/ML test results where applicable.
- **Documents:** 5.10 STP; test procedures; test reports; traceability matrices; system safety data package (hazard reports); defect tracking system.
- **Milestones:** TRR minutes and approvals are listed as supplementary evidence; test readiness is assessed with the PAT-026 and PAT-027 checklists.
- **Small projects:** Small projects test with risk-based priority (safety-critical first, then mission-critical, with some verification of everything), tailoring scope to available simulators and hardware and protecting flight hardware with a simulation-first approach. They still need a lightweight test plan, concise procedures, test logs (build number, environment, pass/fail, issues), reports with defects and corrective actions, configuration-managed test artifacts, and a documented rationale for any reduced rigor.
- **Software assurance:** confirm requirements test coverage through execution of the test procedures; witness tests of safety-critical software; confirm newly identified software contributions to hazards, events or conditions found during testing are added to the system safety data package (NASA-STD-8739.8B, no task IDs given).
- **Also:** 100 percent code coverage is not always feasible and does not prove correctness or validate requirements; focus coverage on functionality, performance and safety, use non-intrusive tools, and run acceptance tests on unmodified production builds. Requirements with multiple logical conditions go through FQT; conditions untestable in FQT are covered by lower-level tests, and exceptions are documented and approved by the software control board. AI/ML software needs added testing (training data, novel inputs, explainability; topics 7.25 and 8.25). SA products (7.2): test witnessing signatures, test coverage metrics, confirmation of hazard resolution testing, reviewed test plans, procedures and reports.

## SWE-068 Evaluate Test Results

%guidance[HB-068 interprets=SWE-068] All test data is compared against the expected results from the test plan or requirements, discrepancies are documented and analyzed, and the evaluation process, criteria and tools are recorded so the evaluation can be repeated and audited. Evaluations trace results to requirements and acceptance criteria, analyze coverage and rigor against risk, resolve or disposition every discrepancy, and give safety-critical results the highest priority, checking that hazards are eliminated or mitigated. The recorded outcome includes anomalies, problem reports, operational difficulties, abnormal terminations, discrepancy justifications, known requirement deficiencies, corrective actions, tests that could not be evaluated and why, and pass/fail status with justification.

- **Objective evidence:** approved software test plan with hazard control strategies and SA roles; test procedures for hazard control verification; hazard test planning artifacts mapping each hazard control to test cases; traceability from safety-critical requirements to hazard controls to test procedures; test execution logs; safety control test results (safe-state transition, stress, edge inputs); high-fidelity simulation or HIL logs; software test reports with hazard-related results and coverage analysis; test coverage reports; discrepancy or test failure logs with root cause, correction and retest; non-conformance or problem reports; defect tracking database; anomaly reports and dispositions; retest evidence with SA approval; completed hazard traceability matrix; hazard closure reports signed by SA; SA test completion sign-off; safety control verification records; SA metrics, dashboards and discrepancy trends; peer review records; IV&V confirmation if applicable.
- **Documents:** software test reports (STRs); problem or non-conformance tracking system; hazard reports; traceability matrices; configuration records of the test context.
- **Milestones:** The guidance notes that untraceable results weaken critical reviews such as the TRR and safety reviews.
- **Small projects:** Small projects tailor test rigor to risk, with safety-critical software always getting full testing and mission-critical functions next, and prefer simulators or models over flight hardware. Documentation stays mandatory: software version tested, test environment configuration, expected and actual results, discrepancies and dispositions, and anomalies with resolutions, with tests designed to be repeatable.
- **Software assurance:** confirm test results are assessed and recorded; confirm software non-conformances are documented in a tracking system; confirm test results are sufficient verification artifacts for the hazard reports (NASA-STD-8739.8B, no task IDs given).
- **Also:** Record the test context for repeatability: test tools, compilers, OS, third-party test software, exact code version (tagged), hardware and simulation environment, and test limitations; keep drivers, stubs, simulators, models, test suites and data under configuration management with versions recorded. Plan evaluation criteria in advance (acceptable output range or accuracy, minimum input and output combinations, allowed test duration, allowed interrupts or halts, allowed error severity, conditions for inconclusive results and retest, conditions indicating bad test data or procedures). Use checklists, domain specialists and automated analysis tools. SA (7.4) signs off on test completion only after all discrepancies are resolved or mitigated, and a hazard verification is complete only when all related issues are resolved. SA products (7.2): software test reports, problem or defect data, test coverage metric data.

## SWE-070 Models, Simulations, Tools

%guidance[HB-070 interprets=SWE-070] Any software model, simulation or analysis tool used to qualify flight software or flight equipment must be verified, validated and accredited (approved for that specific use) before its results are relied on. - **Software assurance:** confirm that the models, simulations and analysis tools used to qualify flight software or flight equipment are validated and accredited (NASA-STD-8739.8B, no task ID given).
- **Also:** Accreditation record should capture purpose and scope of use, decisions driven by results, consequences of erroneous outputs, and constraints and limits. Verification record: techniques, conditions, numerical error estimates, status. Validation record: techniques, conditions, metrics and data sets, studies, results. ## SWE-083 Status Accounting

%guidance[HB-083 interprets=SWE-083] The project keeps configuration status accounting (CSA) records for its CIs throughout the life cycle, and both acquirer and provider should have a status accounting system. Records show each CI's life-cycle stage (draft, under review, ready for integration or delivery, operational, superseded), its current version and change history, check-in/out state, last update time, who changed it and what changed, plus the status of every CR and PR with the CIs affected. The project decides what CSA data it needs (for example which versions are installed where, what differs between versions, which CI revisions make up a product version, errors per version), coordinates it with data management, and records the CSA and data requirements in the SCMP.

- **Objective evidence:** version history records (CI name, version, status, author, date, change description); baseline definitions listing CIs and versions per baseline; release records and VDDs (items and versions, changes, known defects, requirements supported); CR tracking logs with status and affected CIs; PR logs traced to changes; SA audit results of CR and PR processes; version control logs, commit history, branches and tags; baseline tags or snapshots; CI/CD build and release logs; CM audit and process compliance audit reports; signed, approved SCMP with CSA policies, versioning, baselines and roles; periodic CI and baseline status reports, release or integration readiness reports; traceability matrices tying requirements to baselines and versioned CIs; change-to-CI-version traceability
- **Documents:** 5.06 SCMP (CSA and data management requirements); 5.01 CR-PR; VDD (SWE-063, 5.16 implied as version description document); configuration status reports
- **Milestones:** CSA records updated at major milestones (phase transitions) and reported at milestones; VDD for every release or delivery
- **Small projects:** Keep a short CI list and a single CSA log spreadsheet (CI name, version, status, last updated, change summary, responsible person, linked CR or issue), or let GitHub or GitLab history and CI/CD build history serve as the CSA record; one person owns updates, weekly for active projects and at each phase transition, with informal accuracy and completeness checks and a simple release version description per release.
- **Software assurance:** confirm the project maintains records of the configuration status of CIs (8739.8B task 1); SA reviews problem reporting or defect tracking data, SCM system data, SA audit results of change management, and VDDs; sample CSA records against the actual state of software; check for orphaned items; spot-check traceability of changes through CRs and PRs; confirm VDD versions match CSA records; confirm tools capture CSA data automatically and integrate change, defect and version control
- **Also:** CSA record fields per CI: name, version, stage or status, check-in/out state, last update, changer, change summary, linked CR or PR; STEP Level 2 SCM and DM course lists the status accounting questions a project should be able to answer

## SWE-084 Configuration Audits

%guidance[HB-084 interprets=SWE-084] The project manager performs configuration audits that confirm the correct versions of CIs are in a baseline or release and that they match their defining records: a Functional Configuration Audit (FCA) against the functional baseline (test results, V&V reports, approved changes, traceability, all planned testing done) and a Physical Configuration Audit (PCA) against the product baseline (as-built code vs. design, no TBDs, FCA actions resolved, coding standards, user documentation). Rigor scales with phase and criticality: formal releases get formal, comprehensive audits, interim releases less formal ones. The SCMP plans the audits (goals, schedule, participants, contractor role, procedure); audits sample records, use auditors independent of the audited product, and follow plan, prepare, perform, close-out with a report.

- **Objective evidence:** SCMP audit process (roles, scope, frequency, criteria, corrective action handling); FCA report (test and V&V summary, requirements-to-test mapping, non-conformances); PCA report (product vs. baseline comparison, version control compliance, user manuals and release notes); completed FCA and PCA checklists (PAT-037); VDDs listing items and versions, deviations, waivers, open items, and a statement that the release matches its baseline; CI version and traceability records; configuration status accounting reports; PRs raised from audits and linked CRs with closure; test plans, procedures, results and V&V reports; release documentation (user and operator manuals, release notes, customer acceptance); audit metrics (planned vs. actual, non-conformances by type, open vs. closed trends, coverage); SA-reviewed FCA and PCA reports, SA independent audit reports, SA sign-off on the delivery baseline; CM audit training records
- **Documents:** 5.06 SCMP (audit planning); FCA and PCA reports; PAT-037 Configuration Management Process Audit checklist; VDD (5.16 by name "Version Description Document"); NASA Systems Engineering Handbook table of data reviewed
- **Milestones:** functional baseline approved at PDR and CDR; audits at product release, before delivery, at end of life-cycle phases, before baseline updates, and incrementally for large systems with a summary audit at key milestones; PCA against the product baseline at delivery or release
- **Small projects:** Still do both FCA and PCA but limit scope to critical functions and deliverables, possibly combined in one session, using tailored PAT-037 checklists, sample testing, Git logs and spreadsheets; keep the auditor independent of the audited artifact, audit before delivery, after major changes and at phase ends, and report findings (major or minor), positive observations and corrective actions concisely.
- **Software assurance:** confirm the project manager performed configuration audits to determine the correct CI versions and that audit results conform to the defining records (8739.8B task 1); SA products: review of problem and defect tracking data, SCM system data, SA audits of change management, VDD verification, FCA and PCA audit reports, SA sign-off on delivery documentation; SA participates in FCA and PCA, classifies findings major or minor, verifies action items resolved or deferred with approval, signs off delivery documentation
- **Also:** data typically reviewed: functional requirements specs, architectural and detailed design, coding standards and compliance reports, test plans, formal test results, V&V documentation, CRs, change logs, PRs, approved operational and user documents, baseline and build reports; reports separate major findings (significant impact on performance, schedule, cost or safety) from minor ones, include positive observations, corrective actions and follow-up; DoD CM Guidance Handbook (MIL-HDBK-61) cited for audit planning tables

## SWE-085 Release Management

## SWE-086 Continuous Risk Management

%guidance[HB-086 interprets=SWE-086] The software organization keeps and works its own list of software risks for the whole life cycle, even if the project office does not recognize them at project level, following NPR 8000.4 as far as possible; the NPR note adds that managers should know the residual risk after mitigation or acceptance. Each risk is identified (multidisciplinary brainstorming, checklists from 7.19 and 8.24, lessons learned, SA on CCBs, re-evaluation on significant change), recorded with an ID, condition-consequence statement, likelihood, impact, priority and owner in a central tool, analyzed (likelihood 1 to 5, impact matrices), given a strategy (accept, mitigate, avoid, transfer, watch), tracked and controlled through regular risk reviews and watch lists, and communicated to stakeholders. The project manager approves risk plans; a software risk manager documents and tracks; SA monitors and validates, especially safety-critical risks.

- **Objective evidence:** software risk management plan (SRMP); SDP risk sections; tailored Center risk process; selected risk tool or database; risk register or log (condition, consequence, likelihood, impact, status); risk brainstorming notes; risk checklists; IV&V risk surveillance findings; lessons learned citations; probability-impact matrices; PRA, FTA or other risk analyses; software FMEA or FMECA; risk prioritization reports; risk mitigation plans; risk decision logs (accepted, transferred, eliminated); contingency plans; mitigation trigger criteria; risk review minutes; updated risk logs; risk status reports at milestones; watch lists; risk dashboards; risk reports for reviews; stakeholder communication records including contractor risks; SA audit reports of the risk process; SA-submitted risks tracked to closure; SA participation records; risk-related NCRs and issue logs; compliance trend reports; risk metrics (identified, closed, with mitigation, severity trends, by phase); final risk status and closure logs at closeout; risk lessons learned reports
- **Documents:** 5.08 SDP-SMP; software risk management plan; project risk system per NPR 8000.4; 7.19 Software Risk Management Checklists; 8.24 Software Assurance Risk; 5.05 Metrics report
- **Milestones:** risk reviews at project milestones; risk status reports and top-risk summaries at PDR, CDR and TRR; final risk status at project closeout
- **Small projects:** Brainstorm risks at kickoff and status meetings, keep them in a spreadsheet or simple ticket tool with at least risk statement (condition and consequence), probability and impact (high, medium, low), owner and mitigation plan, sort them into critical, monitor and no-action groups, review them weekly, and document the approach in the risk spreadsheet or a lightweight risk plan in the SDP.
- **Software assurance:** confirm and assess that the risk management process records, analyzes, plans, tracks, controls and communicates all software risks and mitigation plans (8739.8B task 1); audit the risk management process for software activities (task 2); SA products: assessment of risk planning in SDP and SRMP, risk management process audit report, software risk documentation, status products (open and mitigated risks, audit results, status charts); SA submits independently found risks to the project risk board and escalates them if not addressed; SA attends risk reviews and peer reviews
- **Also:** risk statement format: condition and consequence; likelihood scale example: 1 low (process prevents) to 4 high (likely) to 5 inevitable; risk categories: cost, schedule, technical, skill; SA checks for documented strategy, mitigation criteria, tailored process, tracking tool, scheduled risk reviews, escalation path and assigned roles; software risks feed cost estimation (SWE-015)

## SWE-087 Software Peer Reviews and Inspections for Requirements, Plans, Design, Code, and Test Procedures

%guidance[HB-087 interprets=SWE-087] The project manager performs and reports peer reviews or inspections of software requirements, software plans (including cybersecurity), design items and code that the software plans designate, and test procedures; NASA-STD-8739.9 (Software Formal Inspection Standard) holds the recommended practices, and the NPR note recommends peer reviews for all safety and mission-success software. A review follows a planned process (planning, overview, preparation, inspection meeting, optional third hour, rework, follow-up) with readiness and completion criteria, artifact-specific checklists, defined roles (moderator, author, reader, recorder, peers, hardware and systems engineers), and action items tracked until the moderator verifies major defects are fixed. Design and code reviews should target high-risk, complex, safety-critical and cybersecurity-relevant areas, and the plans (SDP/SMP) say which design and code items get reviewed.

- **Objective evidence:** peer review schedule; SDP/SMP defining the peer review process and items to review; artifact-specific checklists; participant and training plan; meeting records (agenda, attendance, action items, notes); sign-in logs showing required roles; inspection packages; SA participation records; peer review metrics reports (completed vs. planned, non-conformances open and closed, defect trends, closure time, defects per review and artifact type, percent resolved before next phase); non-conformance tracking logs; SA findings metrics; completed annotated checklists; requirements verification findings; revised work products incorporating action items; action item logs (description, category, priority, assignee, status); defect management records; rework validation reports; safety review evidence; external or IV&V reviewer participation; peer review summary reports for SRR, PDR, CDR; static analysis outputs used in reviews; defect tracker logs; peer review procedures; training records for SA and reviewers
- **Documents:** 5.03 Inspect (software inspection, peer reviews, inspections record); 5.08 SDP-SMP (which items get reviewed); 7.10 Peer Review and Inspections Including Checklists; NASA-STD-8739.9; reviewed products such as 5.09 SRS, 5.13 SwDD, 5.10 STP, 5.14 test procedures
- **Milestones:** peer reviews precede milestone reviews or approval cycles; SA checks review planning early (before SRR or PDR); peer review summary reports prepared for SRR, PDR, CDR and other milestones
- **Small projects:** The inspection team may shrink (for example moderator, author and one technical expert) as long as key stakeholders are represented, with outside experts from interfacing areas, related projects, functional organizations or users, and Center QA as trained moderator if available; always review requirements, focus design and code reviews on critical, complex or changing areas, keep meetings to 1 to 2 hours, combine roles (moderator as recorder), and use static analysis for routine checks.
- **Software assurance:** confirm peer reviews are performed and reported (8739.8B task 1); confirm the project addresses accepted peer review findings (task 2); perform peer reviews on SA and software safety plans (task 3); confirm at each code inspection that source code meets SWE-134 items a through l for applicable safety-critical functionality (task 4); SA products: SA peer review records, peer review metrics, reports and findings, list of participants; SA attends planned reviews, may act as moderator or recorder, classifies code findings with an error taxonomy (algorithm, initialization, checking, interfaces, logic, standards, non-functional, timing), and verifies code reviews confirm code meets requirements
- **Also:** team best practice: at least four inspectors, author not reader, recorder or moderator, moderator not a manager and formally trained, management presence discouraged; meetings under 2 hours; defects not used to rate authors; NPR 7123.1 Table G-19 entrance criteria (product available, independent reviewers chosen, agenda and success criteria agreed, rules set, spectrum considerations per NPD 2570.5) and success criteria (integrity evaluated, defects characterized, results communicated, spectrum concurrence, code review verifies code meets requirements); detailed checklist items given for requirements, architectural design, detailed design, code, test plans and test procedures; NASA-STD-8709.22 definitions of peer review

## SWE-088 Software Peer Reviews and Inspections - Checklist Criteria and Tracking

%guidance[HB-088 interprets=SWE-088] For every planned peer review or inspection the project uses a checklist or formal reading technique (for example perspective-based reading), applies defined readiness (entrance) and completion (exit) criteria, tracks review actions to resolution, and names the required participants. Checklists are tailored to the artifact type and the reviewer perspective, distributed in advance, and evolved from defect history (NASA-STD-8739.9 stresses them). Readiness covers artifact maturity, team preparation and quality thresholds (for example static analysis results and traceability available); completion means defects logged, classified and dispositioned, metrics collected, findings summarized and all planned steps done per the SDP/SMP; action items live in a central tracker and fixes are independently verified before closure; participants are chosen for expertise, diversity and independence from the author.

- **Objective evidence:** peer review plan or schedule with roles and planned work products; entrance and exit criteria per review; work product type record; participant list with roles and qualifications; checklists used and their version history; completed checklists; proof materials were distributed in advance and reviewers prepared (annotations, invitations); meeting minutes and attendance; defect logs (description, major or minor class, assignee, status, detection and closure dates); defect trend reports; completed entrance and exit criteria checklists (defects logged, metrics collected, re-inspection decision, actions dispositioned); peer review metrics reports (defects by type, per work product, per participant, time to close, preparation and meeting effort); action item logs with closure evidence; SA verification of action item closure; open vs. closed action dashboards; peer review process audit reports and responses; compliance records against NASA-STD-8739.9 and NPR 7150.2; participant role and qualification records; lessons learned and checklist or procedure updates; tool outputs (static analysis, defect tracker, metrics)
- **Documents:** 5.03 Inspect (peer review record); 5.08 SDP-SMP (review steps); 7.10 Peer Review and Inspections Including Checklists; NASA-STD-8739.9
- **Small projects:** Use lightweight checklists focused on high-risk areas (Fraunhofer Center checklists as a starting point), split checklist sections among team members, and use free or low-cost tools (Jira, Bugzilla, Redmine, GitHub code review, Crucible, SonarQube, or spreadsheets) to log defects from detection to closure and produce simple metrics reports.
- **Software assurance:** confirm the project meets NPR criteria a through d for each peer review (8739.8B task 1); confirm the project resolves review actions (task 2); audit the peer review process (task 3); SA products: peer review process audit report (yearly), peer review metrics and data, participant lists with roles, defect tracking data; SA independently tracks action items and verifies closure, and holds follow-up with the review lead
- **Also:** optimal team size 5 to 9 participants, excluding direct managers of the artifact; NPR 7123.1 Table G-19 entrance and success criteria repeated (spectrum per NPD 2570.5); tracker fields: defect description, priority, assignee, resolution status, closure date; annual SA audit of the peer review process; the page's SA and Objective Evidence text refers to "SWE-087" where it means this requirement

## SWE-089 Software Peer Reviews and Inspections - Basic Measurements

%guidance[HB-089 interprets=SWE-089] For every planned peer review or inspection the project records the needed measurements, following NASA-STD-8739.9 practice: effort (planning and preparation, meeting time, rework), participants and roles, defects found and corrected by class (major, minor), size planned and actually inspected, and the product appraisal (accepted, conditionally accepted, re-review). The project manager sets the format and storage location, moderators compile and report results, and SA checks consistency; units and defect categories are defined once and used throughout, outliers are investigated, and data are analyzed periodically (planned vs. completed reviews, inspection rate, defect density and closure time) to improve checklists, training and targeting. - **Objective evidence:** peer review plans (artifacts, schedule, goals, roles, entrance and exit criteria, checklists); agendas; reviewed artifacts and readiness checks; reworked artifacts; attendance records with roles and expertise and preparation completion; invitations; moderator reports; meeting minutes; defect logs (severity, description, location, who found it); metrics reports (reviews conducted vs. planned, defects by type and severity, defect density trends, preparation and meeting time, per-reviewer preparation effort, open vs. closed defects, SA-found defects); non-conformance reports with assignee, status and time to resolve; closure and re-review evidence; defect aggregation reports; completed signed checklists; SA audit reports of the peer review process and SA defect data; lessons learned and process change records; historical trend reports; approved tailoring of the peer review process; compliance reports against NASA-STD-8739.9; SDP or SAP alignment; training and preparation records; summary dashboards and management status reports
- **Documents:** 5.03 Inspect (inspection record); 7.10 Peer Review and Inspections Including Checklists; 5.08 SDP-SMP or SA plan for alignment; contract metric clauses in acquisitions
- **Milestones:** data collected after each inspection meeting and analyzed periodically (monthly or per milestone) and at audits
- **Small projects:** Use 3 to 5 reviewers including at least one SA or independent reviewer, lightweight readiness and exit checks, concise checklists from SPAN, free tools (GitHub Issues, Jira, Excel), and a minimal metric set: defects by severity, open vs. closed defects, aggregate preparation and review time, participants vs. required, and artifacts reviewed vs. planned; SA may act as moderator or observer.
- **Software assurance:** confirm the project records peer review and inspection measurements (8739.8B task 1); SA products: peer review metrics, reports and findings, participant lists (name, role, functional area), defect tracking data with SA-found defects prioritized, SA audit reports (entrance and exit criteria, checklist use, readiness), non-conformance closure reports; SA records its own hours (preparation, meeting, follow-up) and its findings vs. other reviewers to show SA value
- **Also:** base metrics table: size planned and actual (LOC or pages), meeting time, planning effort, meeting effort (meeting time times participants), rework effort, major and minor defects found and corrected, number of inspectors (excluding observers), product appraisal; derived metric: defects per peer review = total defects / number of peer reviews; time-box sessions to 2 hours; several passages say "SWE-087" where they mean this requirement

## SWE-134 Safety-Critical Software Design Requirements

%guidance[HB-134 interprets=SWE-134] A project with safety-critical or mission-critical software must build twelve design features into it: (a) safe initialization at first start and restart, (b) safe transitions between predefined states, (c) termination to a safe state, (d) operator overrides needing at least two independent operator actions, (e) rejection of hazardous out-of-sequence commands, (f) detection of and recovery from inadvertent memory modification, (g) integrity checks on inputs and outputs, (h) prerequisite checks before safety-critical commands, (i) no single software event or action can initiate an identified hazard, (j) response to off-nominal conditions within the time needed to prevent a hazard, (k) error handling, (l) ability to put the system in a safe state. Per the NPR note these apply to components in a mission- or safety-critical system that control, mitigate or contribute to a hazard, and to software that commands hazardous operations. Guidance details each item (for example hardware self-test and memory checks at boot, EDC/ECC, CRC, scrubbing and partitioning for memory, input range and output sanity checks, command gating, real-time timing analysis, defined safe states such as disarmed, standby or power off) and adds design rules for safety-critical code.

- **Objective evidence:** per item a to l: design description of safe initialization and cold and warm start test results; state transition diagrams, transition tests and simulations; termination requirements and tests; SRS override logic, dual-action override tests and operator procedures; command sequence design and rejection tests and logs; memory integrity design, fault-injection test results and runtime check logs; input and output validation logic, tests and boundary analysis; prerequisite check design, tests and code inspection reports; FMEA and redundancy analysis showing no single event initiates a hazard, with tests; timing requirements and response latency tests and real-time simulations; error handling design, logs and tests; safe-state entry logic and safe-state tests and simulations; code review reports confirming implementation
- **Documents:** 5.09 SRS; 5.13 SwDD; test plans and reports; hazard analyses and FMEA; PAT-035 Checklist for Safety-Critical or Mission-Critical Software; requirements mapping matrices signed by engineering and SMA TAs; 8.04; 7.23; 7.24
- **Milestones:** SA assesses source code against items a to l at each code inspection, test review, safety review and project review milestone
- **Small projects:** Define safe states early, keep design simple and deterministic, automate detection, recovery and safe-state transitions, and use hardware features (parity, ECC, watchdog timers) and existing libraries; simple per-item measures include default non-hazardous startup, two-step confirmations, range checks on inputs and outputs, mode checks before commands, timers for mitigation deadlines, cyclomatic complexity under 10, no recursion or goto, no dynamic allocation, checked return values and tested nominal and off-nominal paths.
- **Software assurance:** analyze software requirements and design and work with the project to implement items a to l (8739.8B task 1); assess source code against items a to l at each code inspection, test review, safety review and project review milestone (task 2); confirm safety-critical loaded data, uplinked data, rules and scripts affecting hazardous behavior are tested (task 3); analyze the design for partitioning or isolation and logical isolation of safety-critical elements and data (task 4); participate in reviews affecting safety-critical products (task 5); ensure the SWE-134 implementation supports and is consistent with the system hazard analysis (task 6); SA products: SA status reports, software design analysis, source code analysis, verification activities analysis, evidence of testing safety-critical data, rules and scripts, signed RMMs with traceability from items a to l to design, code and tests
- **Also:** design rules in guidance: review code with cyclomatic complexity above 15 (small-projects tab suggests below 10), no recursion, goto or unbounded loops, no runtime heap allocation, at least two runtime assertions per function, limited pointer use, all compiler warnings enabled and resolved; program safety requirements take priority over software features when they conflict; failure philosophy trade-offs (fault tolerance, control-path separation) are documented; SWE-087 SA task 4 checks code against these items at each code inspection

## SWE-135 Static Analysis

%guidance[HB-135 interprets=SWE-135] The project uses static analysis tools on the code during development and testing to detect, at a minimum, defects, security weaknesses, code coverage and complexity; the NPR note says all projects should collect and use complexity metrics even though no maximum applies to non-safety-critical code, while safety-critical software must meet the cyclomatic complexity (3.7.5, SWE-220) and code coverage (3.7.4, SWE-219) requirements. Tools are configured for the project's coding standards (for example MISRA-C, CERT C), tuned to limit false positives and ranked by severity, run quick scans routinely and precise scans before milestones, and their results feed code peer reviews (SWE-087). Safety- or mission-critical code should use sound and complete analyzers; where no tool exists for a language, documented manual review procedures replace it and are recorded in the compliance matrix and project plan.

- **Objective evidence:** tool selection and configuration records (name, version, languages, rationale, licensing, enabled checkers and filters); compatibility assessment against coding standards; raw tool outputs and summary reports (counts, categories, severity); periodic trend runs; issue tracking records (severity, type, status: addressed, deferred, false positive, under review) with traceability to resolution; resolution logs for critical and high issues; justifications for deferred items or waivers; coding standard enforcement records and violation reports with approved exceptions; security analysis reports and cybersecurity risk assessment; metrics (errors and warnings found, evaluated vs. total, closure ratios by severity, trends, vulnerabilities open vs. closed, secure coding compliance); cyclomatic complexity reports (SWE-220); code coverage reports (SWE-219); audit reports and SWE-219/220 compliance checklists; code peer review logs including static analysis results; IV&V assessments; tool evaluation and training records; waiver and exception records; final static analysis certification report signed by SA, IV&V or the project
- **Documents:** compliance matrix (for alternate methods where no tool exists); project plan (5.08 SDP-SMP implied as "project plan"); 8.26 Static Analysis; 8.19 Dead/Dormant Code; NASA IV&V tools table
- **Milestones:** thorough scans before major milestones; for small teams, at feature complete, before integration testing and before final delivery; final certification before delivery
- **Small projects:** Start with essential checks (security, major standards violations, high complexity) using free tools (Clang-Tidy, Cppcheck, Pylint, Bandit, SpotBugs, PMD, SonarQube Community), run them in CI on each commit or nightly with default rules first, triage by severity, document suppressions, and if no tool exists for the language record an alternate manual process in the compliance matrix with extra focus on safety-critical code; NASA IV&V can provide tools.
- **Software assurance:** analyze engineering data or perform independent static analysis for defects, quality objectives, coverage, complexity and security (8739.8B task 1); confirm tools are used with checkers for security and coding errors (task 2); assess that the project addresses static analysis results from SA, safety, engineering or the project (task 3); confirm code was scanned for security defects and confirm the result (task 4); for safety-critical software verify code coverage and approved waivers per SWE-219 (task 5) and cyclomatic complexity and approved waivers per SWE-220 (task 6); confirm software quality objectives or thresholds are set for static analysis defects, checks or security (task 7); SA products: static analysis results tracked as corrective actions with severity, category, phase and status, integrated across engineering, SA and IV&V
- **Also:** critical security and safety findings must be resolved before deployment; unresolved vulnerabilities need documented, approved risk justification; triage order: critical security, safety, maintainability, cosmetic; fix early-execution findings first and rerun to remove cascades; give the analyzer the same compiler settings and libraries as the build

## SWE-136 Software Tool Accreditation

%guidance[HB-136 interprets=SWE-136] The project manager validates and accredits the software tools needed to develop or maintain the software so that tools do not insert errors into executables; per the NPR note, accreditation certifies a tool as acceptable for a specific purpose and is conferred by the organization best placed to judge. Validation runs representative and edge-case test cases through the tool and compares outputs with expected results; accreditation then formally accepts the validated tool for its project purpose (by the project and software TA, an approved external body such as NIST, or a vendor with a proven process) and is recorded in the SMP/SDP under configuration control. Direct tools (compilers, coverage tools, IDEs, build tools, code generators, math and linked libraries, debuggers, static analyzers) need it; indirect tools (bug trackers, metrics or document generators) may be accepted as validated by use. The flow is: inventory tools, check existing accreditation applies, validate as needed, approve and document (software TA sign-off), and monitor and revalidate on upgrades or phase transitions; novel tool combinations may need integration-level accreditation.

- **Objective evidence:** tool inventory (name, version, vendor, use, inputs and outputs, role) in the SMP/SDP; tool validation test plans; validation test results (expected vs. actual, repeatability, stress, boundary and exception tests); accreditation records (authority, requirements met, accredited use cases, versions and environment); tool risk assessments (failure modes, severity, likelihood, mitigations) in the risk plan or hazard reports; tool configuration management records (installs, patches with revalidation, library versions, config files); third-party certification or vendor validation evidence; peer review records of validation and accreditation; tool issue logs, root cause and revalidation records; tool-to-work-product traceability matrix; software TA review and approval records
- **Documents:** 5.08 SDP-SMP (tool list and accreditation results); validation plan and tool validation report; risk management plan or hazard reports; configuration management system
- **Milestones:** tools reviewed at major upgrades and life-cycle phase transitions; SA metric tracks tools validated before major milestones
- **Small projects:** Reuse tools and environments already accredited on prior NASA projects (found through project reports, PALs and technology reports), confirm the prior accreditation context matches the new project's needs, scale and life cycle, run high-level validation with reference test cases from earlier projects, record it in the SMP under CM, and peer review the results before formally accepting the tool; adapt prior accreditation procedures when adopting comparable new tools.
- **Software assurance:** confirm the software tools needed to create and maintain software are validated and accredited (8739.8B task 1); SA products: validation and accreditation criteria for tools (safety, reliability, performance, compatibility, CM, defect tracking, testability), validation and accreditation results (test plans, cases, results, acceptance decisions, known limitations with mitigations); SA obtains the tool list under CM, checks existing validation and third-party certification, weighs tool safety impact (SWE-020, SWE-023), confirms outputs match references and tools run within limits, confirms TA approval, and has validation results peer reviewed
- **Also:** validation and accreditation techniques: functional demonstrations, formal and peer reviews, simulation, review of tool-generated artifacts, static analysis of tools and outputs, repeatable automated stress tests; code generator output must itself be validated; licenses of COTS tools must stay valid for the project duration; known tool limitations go in the risk system

## SWE-143 Software Architecture Review

%guidance[HB-143 interprets=SWE-143] The project manager holds a software architecture review on Category 1 projects (NPR 7120.5) and on Category 2 projects with Class A or B payload risk (NPR 8705.4). The review may be a peer review or part of a milestone review (for example MDR/SDR) but must follow the peer review rules of SWE-088 and SWE-089: named participants, a checklist for evaluating the architecture description, readiness and completion criteria, actions tracked to closure, and recorded measurements. It is held during architecture formulation, before the architecture is baselined, generally between MDR/SDR and PDR. - **Objective evidence:** architecture review plan (purpose, scope, schedule, participants, entry/exit and success criteria, references to NPR 7120.5, 7150.2, 8705.4); review schedule tied to milestones (before software PDR); architecture review checklists; meeting agendas, minutes and attendance lists showing required stakeholders; architecture artifacts presented (Software Architecture Description per SWE-057 with diagrams, decomposition and dependency maps, interface definitions, fault management description); traceability matrices from architecture to requirements, safety-critical requirements and hazards; alternatives analysis report; architecture risk logs and mitigation plans; safety hazard assessments tied to architecture elements; review results document with readiness assessment for the next phase; action item and issue log; review metrics data; risk and issue closure records showing findings resolved, mitigated or accepted with justification before the next phase; CM records of the approved architecture baseline and archived review deliverables; safety follow-up verifying alignment with hazard analyses.
- **Documents:** Software Architecture Description (SWE-057); architecture review plan or integrated life-cycle review plan; architecture review results report; action item and issue log; problem tracking or corrective action system; 7.10 peer review checklists; CM repository.
- **Milestones:** Held before the architecture is baselined, generally between MDR/SDR and PDR (see 7.09); SA guidance and evidence place it before software PDR; earlier reviewer involvement is more effective.
- **Small projects:** Small projects that fall under the criteria can run a lightweight, risk-driven review (workshops or small peer reviews, possibly incremental) focused on structure, safety, interfaces and resource constraints. Key artifacts: high-level architecture diagram, requirements-to-architecture traceability matrix, interface definitions, architectural risk mitigation reports and a short findings report; track risks identified vs. resolved, requirements coverage and safety-critical issues.
- **Software assurance:** assess the results of, or take part in, the project's software architecture review activities (NASA-STD-8739.8B task 1); SA products: software architecture review assessment report; SA status report on review participation and open risks; assessment of architecture review results and adequacy of corrective actions; architecture risk and issue log including how hazards are mitigated; software design analysis results; review findings and resolution status; SA guidance: take part in formulation and review, make sure the review happens before software PDR, apply checklist areas (requirements and quality attributes, component sources and COTS rationale, documentation and traceability, fault management and complexity, performance and scalability, safety-critical components traced to hazards), confirm minimum architecture documentation, treat architecture as iterative.
- **Also:** Participants represent key stakeholders (computing hardware engineers, interfacing systems, testers, installers, users, data suppliers and consumers); SWE-088 suggests at least three reviewers. Independent reviewers are not required; the NASA Software Architecture Review Board (SARB) can be asked for one, and its checklists are on NEN (PAT-023, PAT-029, PAT-030). Evaluation criteria cited: ISO/IEC 12207 6.4.3.3.2.1 (traceability, consistency, appropriate design methods, feasibility of items and of operation and maintenance) and an OSMA question list. Minimum architecture documentation: assessment of alternatives, description of chosen architecture, subsystem decomposition, dependencies between subsystems, methods to measure and verify conformance, inherent risks, rationale for changes, evaluation and impact of proposed changes. Reviewers should note whether a weakness lies in the architecture or in its description; measurements follow SWE-089.

## SWE-146 Auto-generated Source Code

%guidance[HB-146 interprets=SWE-146] The project manager documents the project's approach to auto-generated source code, covering all seven items a to g: V&V of the generation tools, CM of the tools and their data, limits and allowable scope (with rationale), V&V of generated code to the same standards and processes as hand-written code, monitoring planned vs. actual use, policies for manual changes, and CM of tool inputs, outputs and modifications. Tools are validated before use and again for each new version or reconfiguration (SWE-136), and generated code is verified like hand code since generators are typically not qualified (SWE-027). The approach is recorded in the SDP, the CM plan and V&V documentation.

- **Objective evidence:** (a) tool validation report, tool test results, tool verification checklist, independent tool assessment report; (b) CM plan covering tools and data, tool version logs, change requests, tool repository access logs; (c) scope document for generated code, tool usage guidelines, decision memo with rationale, risk analysis; (d) test plans and procedures for generated code, test results and reports, code review records, regression test results; (e) planned vs. actual usage report, scope change log, metrics report of generated vs. hand-written code, usage analysis memo; (f) manual code change policy, code modification logs with justification and author, approval records from the project manager or technical authority for each manual change, test plans for modified code; (g) CM plan for inputs, outputs and modifications, version control logs, change request records, baseline tracking reports, tool input and output validation records.
- **Documents:** SDP (approach and planned scope); software CM plan (SCMP) covering tools, inputs, outputs and modified code; V&V plans and documentation for generated code; SA plan, V&V plans and software test reports for the SA process; 8.11 Auto-Generated Code for practices.
- **Small projects:** Scale the approach: document scope, tools and manual change policy briefly in the SDP, pick well-established generators and validate them cheaply (prototype runs, vendor results), keep input models and tool settings in version control, treat fully generated code as disposable while recording how to regenerate it (model and tool versions), and test generated code like hand code. Keep a simple log of where generated code is used compared with the plan.
- **Software assurance:** assess that the auto-generation approach is defined and satisfies at least conditions a through g (NASA-STD-8739.8B task 1); SA products: assessment of the software engineering plans against items a to g with issues, risks and gaps recorded; evaluation of the approach in the SDP; check that the SCMP covers inputs, tools, outputs and manually modified generated code and says when outputs need CM; SA guidance: verify tools are validated per version (SWE-136 or equivalent) and check known tool issues, confirm CM traceability from models to code, review scope rationale against safety classification and risk tolerance, confirm generated code gets static analysis and requirements-based testing like hand code, check planned vs. actual use against the SDP and that deviations are risk-assessed, review manual change policies and that edits are fed back into models, examine the maturity at which artifacts enter CM.
- **Also:** Items under CM: input models and data, generation tools and versions, initialization and tool configuration scripts, reference models used to validate the tool, other data needed to run the tool, any modified intermediate output, and manually modified generated code. The approach states the model maturity at which models enter CM and whether generated code follows its own or hand-code CM rules. The page is internally inconsistent on output CM: the guidance says 100% generated code is typically not configuration managed (it can be reproduced), while item g of the requirement calls for CM of tool output. Scope may be set by safety-criticality, complexity, ability to segregate generated from hand code, and cost-benefit. For AI software see 7.25 and 8.25.

Guidance says NASA-STD-1006 has four objectives but lists three.

## SWE-185 Secure Coding Standards Verification

%guidance[HB-185 interprets=SWE-185] The project manager verifies that the code meets the project's secure coding standard using static analysis tool results; where no static analysis tool works with the chosen standard, manual inspection or other methods are acceptable (NPR note). ## SWE-192 Software Hazardous Requirements

%guidance[HB-192 interprets=SWE-192] Software requirements that trace to a hazardous event, cause or mitigation (controls, inhibits, mitigations) are verified by test, not by analysis, demonstration or inspection alone. Test cases derive from system safety analyses (PHA, FMEA, FTA, subsystem hazard analyses), hazard control documentation and the traceability matrix, and cover nominal, abnormal (bad commands, invalid inputs, hardware failures) and off-nominal conditions, including FDIR, safe-state transitions and safeguards against hazardous commands. Test procedures, test software and hardware and results are under configuration management, and safety tests are rerun as regression after changes.

- **Objective evidence:** requirements traceability matrix (hazard to requirement to test to result); completed hazard requirements flow-down matrix (hazard, cause, software function, requirement, test procedure and result); test plan for safety-critical software; test procedures including fault injection; test results reports and test logs or data; SA approval signatures and review or inspection reports; code coverage (MC/DC) reports and tool output; simulation, fault injection and safe-state test results; hazard verification metrics; non-conformance and corrective action reports; configuration management logs and change history; IV&V test results and sign-off where used; lessons learned report
- **Documents:** test plan (SWE-065; small projects: STP listing safety-critical functions and how each is tested); verification matrix linking safety-critical requirements to tests; hazard requirements flow-down matrix; 8.05 software FMEA; 8.01 off-nominal testing
- **Small projects:** Identify hazard-related software from PHA or FMEA, write a small STP with nominal, off-nominal and edge-case tests, and keep a simple spreadsheet RTM (requirement, hazard, test case, result). MC/DC is the standard for safety-critical paths; if 100% is not reachable, document the rationale and focus on high-risk and hazard-control logic; use simulation or software-in-the-loop where hardware testing is unsafe or costly.
- **Software assurance:** through testing, confirm the project verifies software requirements that trace to a hazardous event, cause or mitigation technique (NASA-STD-8739.8B; single task, no number given). SA products: hazard analysis artifacts and hazard requirements flow-down matrix; test reports for safety-critical requirements; traceability evidence; SA approval or sign-off on test plans, procedures, cases and results; safety-critical software assessments (software FTA, timing and throughput analyses). SA guidance also has SA formally approve safety-critical test procedures and witness hazard-related tests.
- **Also:** every test maps to one or more requirements, with bidirectional traceability; test hazardous commands against inadvertent operator action (HR-33), corrupted data, and concurrency and timing faults; test hazardous conditions first in simulation, dry runs and fault injection before integrated hardware; SA requirements-phase analyses named: safety requirements flow-down, requirements criticality analysis, specification analysis, timing and throughput, preliminary FTA.

## SWE-193 Acceptance Testing for Affected System and Software Behavior

%guidance[HB-193 interprets=SWE-193] The project develops special acceptance tests for any data, rules (decision logic, autonomy rules, command sequences) and code that is loaded or uplinked and affects software or system behavior, validating them for nominal and off-nominal scenarios (malformed, out-of-range, corrupted or concurrent uplinks). Acceptance testing is system level, normally during final integration of formally tested software with flight or operations hardware, before project-level acceptance, against acceptance criteria set early in Formulation (SWE-034) and refined through the life cycle. Test data loads and configuration values are validated (type, range, format, schema), version controlled and baselined, and results are documented in an STR or acceptance data package and compared with expected results within agreed tolerances.

- **Objective evidence:** acceptance test plan covering uplinked data and configuration scenarios; acceptance test procedures, peer reviewed and approved; test traceability matrix; test execution logs; signed SA observation records of test sessions; software test report reviewed and approved by SA; defect logs with retests; signed test completion certificate; configuration files with version history; validation test results for uplinked data, rules and scripts; configuration management audit reports; data integrity check results; SA review reports; configuration change records; verification and validation reports; non-compliance reports with corrective actions; test metrics dashboard; defect and configuration management metrics; off-nominal test results; simulated environment reports; configuration management plan; configuration audits; baseline approval documents
- **Documents:** 5.08 SDP-SMP or software V&V plan (initial acceptance criteria); 5.11 STR or acceptance data package (objectives and criteria, approach, results per test, deviations and follow-up, acceptance recommendation); software verification reports; configuration management plan for uplinked data, rules and scripts
- **Milestones:** acceptance criteria defined early in Formulation; acceptance concludes with a formal System Acceptance Review (SAR) in Implementation, with entrance and exit criteria per 7.09
- **Small projects:** The tab quotes guidance to run acceptance tests for nominal scenarios on final hardware against the input data and conditions that affect system behavior; validation tests may serve as acceptance tests, and results are documented and evaluated against expected results for customer acceptance. It suggests testing only nominal scenarios unless specific risks are flagged, with spreadsheets for results and simple version control tying results to the tested versions.
- **Software assurance:** confirm the project develops acceptance tests for loaded or uplinked data, rules and code that affect behavior; confirm such data, rules, scripts or code are baselined in the software configuration system; confirm loaded or uplinked data, rules and scripts are verified correct before operations, especially safety-critical operations (NASA-STD-8739.8B; no task numbers given). SA products: software test reports; software test procedures; software configuration data; software risk assessments (optional).
- **Also:** tests run on the intended mission hardware and environment where possible; independent testers preferred, with SA observing; acceptance outcome may be accept, reject or conditional acceptance with known risks, with thresholds agreed up front with stakeholders; uplink protocols (encryption, checksums) and state-dependent loads are tested; unexpected configurations should default to safe parameters; post-deployment changes to uplinked items follow the same CM, with each change traceable to an approved change request; references NPR 7123.1 and NASA-SP-2007-6105.

## SWE-194 Delivery Requirements Verification

- **Software assurance:** confirm the project identified the requirements, approved changes and defects for each delivery; confirm all requirements identified for delivery are met; confirm requirements once planned but no longer in delivery documentation are dispositioned; confirm approved changes are implemented and tested; confirm approved changes and designated defects are resolved; approve or sign off on delivered products (NASA-STD-8739.8B tasks 1 to 6, as listed). ## SWE-195 Software Maintenance Phase

%guidance[HB-195 interprets=SWE-195] Once classification is set (SWE-020), the standards and processes for that class are defined in the SDP/SMP (or a separate software maintenance plan) and the software is maintained with them for its whole maintenance phase, or until its classification changes. Maintenance (corrective, adaptive, perfective, preventive) revisits earlier phases as needed: requirements and traceability updated, SDD updated, coding standards and CM (SWE-109) followed, changes analyzed to set the level of regression testing, and releases controlled with release notes and updated VDDs. Testing in maintenance covers functional, regression, performance, interface and, for safety-critical software, hazard testing, with rigor per class.

- **Objective evidence:** signed and approved SDP/SMP defining maintenance standards and processes per class; updated and signed software maintenance plan; SA audit reports; signed change requests with impact analysis and risk assessment; CM records (baselines, tagged releases, rollback records); defect resolution records; regression testing reports; safety-critical test plans and results; test results for maintenance changes; updated SRS and traceability matrix; updated SDD; updated test plans and procedures; release notes; compliance checklists; SA audit findings and resolutions; performance metrics reports; CM system output; deployment and transition records; operational testing results; risk register; impact analysis results; maintenance phase metrics report; software process metrics; audit findings log and meeting minutes; stakeholder acceptance records
- **Documents:** 5.08 SDP-SMP or 5.04 Software Maintenance Plan (standards and processes); 7.18 documentation guidance; SRS; SDD; test plans, procedures and reports; VDD and release notes; CM plan (SWE-109)
- **Small projects:** Use a scaled-down subset of standards, with lightweight templates (maintenance log, change control record, risk register), a single requirement change log, Git with change requests tied to commits and tags, and risk-based, automated regression testing. The tab gives a compliance checklist: classification applied, maintenance artifacts documented, regression and CM in place, changes approved and traced, risks tracked, documentation updated proportionally.
- **Software assurance:** perform audits on the standards and processes used throughout maintenance, based on the software classification (NASA-STD-8739.8B; single task, no number given). SA product: standards and processes audit report with findings, non-conformances, corrective actions and recommendations, covering CM, change control, safety-critical testing, regression testing and operational transition.
- **Also:** audit findings go into a formal tracking system with description, severity, actions, owner, due date and status, with safety-critical findings escalated promptly; small-project maintenance log records what changed, why (with risk assessment) and who approved; lightweight change workflow: request, impact analysis, approval by team lead or project manager, implement and verify.

## SWE-196 Software Retirement Archival

%guidance[HB-196 interprets=SWE-196] Retirement procedures are documented in the software maintenance plan or SDP/SMP per NPR 7150.2 and NPR 1441.1 (records retention), naming the records and tools to archive (code, executables, libraries, SRS, SDD, test plans and results, manuals, release notes, CM and defect records, compilers, test frameworks and other tools), the archive location, and access procedures (authentication, authorized roles, handling of sensitive or export-controlled items), with roles for the project manager, technical POC, SA and Center CIO. Retirement is triggered by end of life, loss of vendor support, obsolescence, mission completion, cost or security risk. The guidance steps are: notify the Center CIO; identify items; define archive and access; stop all use and remove installs; update the Center inventory (date, reason, archive responsibility); evaluate reuse; dispose of non-archived items securely; validate and report completion.

- **Objective evidence:** software maintenance plan with retirement procedures; SDP/SMP retirement tasks, timelines and roles; data management plan listing archive items, repositories and access controls; archival and disposal procedures including license handling; records retention plan aligned with NPR 1441.1; inventory of archived items with metadata (file name, type, location, date); archive confirmation report; archiving sign-offs by project manager, technical lead and SA; repository directory snapshots; secure disposal records (certificates of destruction, deletion logs); license management logs; SA disposal verification report; retirement notices to stakeholders; licensing closure correspondence; SA audit report; verification checklists; gap analysis results; retention compliance confirmation; access logs; cybersecurity validation of archive storage; periodic availability checks; knowledge sharing or handoff records
- **Documents:** 5.04 Software Maintenance Plan or 5.08 SDP-SMP (retirement procedures); data management plan; records retention plan per NPR 1441.1; retirement closeout package; small projects: retirement completion memo; 7.18 documentation guidance
- **Small projects:** Keep a simple inventory spreadsheet of items to archive or dispose, use Center-managed storage with named people who may access it, have one person confirm cessation with a checklist, and record disposal in a short memo. Put the procedures in the SMP or SDP/SMP and finish with a retirement completion memo giving archive location, archived items, disposal methods and access procedures.
- **Software assurance:** confirm the project has identified the records and software tools for archival; confirm the project archives all software and records selected for archival, as planned (NASA-STD-8739.8B; no task numbers given). - **Also:** retired licenses may be pooled for reassignment; reusable software may go to the NASA software reuse pool or Open Source Software Catalog, otherwise the decommission decision is documented; disposal follows federal erasure standards and records software type, security classification and method; SA validates that archived items can be retrieved with the documented procedures and may check the archive periodically after retirement.

## SWE-199 Performance Measures

%guidance[HB-199 interprets=SWE-199] The project manager monitors measures throughout the life cycle showing the software will meet or exceed its functional and performance requirements and constraints. Per the NPR note, this includes planned vs actual use of hardware resources (processor, memory, I/O, auxiliary storage, network capacity, bus traffic, partition allocation) over time, with estimates updated during detailed design verification and measured values compared to margins during coding, testing and validation. Functional progress is tracked through unit testing, dry-run verification and formal verification against a traceability matrix; performance through peak and sustained processing, response time, storage, sampling rates and CPU and memory use, with results reported at milestone reviews and deficiencies driving corrective action.

- **Objective evidence:** system and software requirements documents with measurable functional and performance requirements; requirements verification matrix (planned verification, test cases and results, coverage status); requirements change logs; test plans and procedures with pass/fail criteria; test data and reports at all levels; test coverage reports; test defect reports with closure records; software metrics plan (metrics, baseline thresholds, schedule, analysis); metrics dashboards and reports; deviation or variance logs; risk management plan; risk logs and status reports; corrective action logs; review records (PDR, CDR, TRR); peer review findings; audit reports; performance test reports and logs; resource utilization records; CM plan and logs; change requests and approvals; lessons learned and post-mortem reports
- **Documents:** software metrics plan; requirements verification matrix; test plans and reports; 5.04 Software Maintenance Plan (retesting in maintenance); 8.52 SA status reports
- **Milestones:** performance results shared at major milestone reviews (PDR, CDR, TRR, ORR); technical resource estimates updated at detailed design verification and measured values at coding, test and validation; retested for maintenance upgrades
- **Small projects:** Track a small set of key metrics: requirements verified vs total, issues found and resolved, and at least one key performance metric tied to the system's purpose, using spreadsheets and simple benchmarks. Example CubeSat case: CPU under 50% at peak transmission, checked with monitoring tools during integration, reported monthly; use a short checklist (functional, performance, constraints, test completion, risk) for milestone reviews.
- **Software assurance:** confirm the project monitors and updates planned measurements to ensure the software meets or exceeds performance and functionality requirements, including constraints; monitor and track any performance or functionality requirements not being met or at risk (NASA-STD-8739.8B; no task numbers given). SA products: independent analysis of software measures; validation of measurement data; trend and analysis reports; status presentations.
- **Also:** performance metrics named: CPU utilization (average, max), memory (MB, percent), throughput (transactions per second), response time (ms), error and retry rates, latency variability; SA must analyze metrics independently, not rely only on engineering assessments; examples cite thresholds such as at most 80% CPU at maximum throughput and 100% of high-priority requirements verified.

## SWE-202 Software Severity Levels

%guidance[HB-202 interprets=SWE-202] The project defines clear, project-tailored severity levels (typically 3 to 5) and applies them to every software non-conformance, including tools, COTS, GOTS, MOTS, OSS, reused components and applicable ground systems. Per the NPR note, the levels must at minimum cover loss of life or vehicle, mission success, user-visible with operational workaround, and an "other" class. Definitions are written into project documents (CM plan, SA documents, test plans), embedded in the defect tracking tool, defined before testing begins, trained across teams and refined from metrics and lessons learned; known defects in third-party software are researched and assigned project-context severity.

- **Objective evidence:** severity definitions document in PMP/SMP, risk management plan, SA plan or test plans; defect tracking tool configuration showing severity categories; exported reports by severity; assessments mapping vendor severity to project severity for COTS, GOTS, MOTS, OSS and reuse; defect and non-conformance reports with severity; test reports and discrepancy logs with severity justification; post-test review records; SA review records and checklists; non-conformance tracking and metrics reports; milestone packages (PDR, CDR, TRR) with non-conformance data; optional dashboards; SCM records, VDDs and CR/PRs by severity; SCB minutes on high-severity items; change management audit logs; annotated vendor defect lists with risk assessments; lessons learned on severity refinement; SA reports and reviews
- **Documents:** CM plan; SA plan; test plans and procedures; PMP/SMP; risk management plan; defect tracking tool; VDD; 5.01 CR-PR
- **Milestones:** severity levels defined and documented before any testing begins; non-conformance severity data presented at PDR, CDR and TRR
- **Small projects:** Use three or four levels (critical, major, minor, optional editorial or trivial) kept in a spreadsheet or ticket labels, with one person maintaining the log. - **Software assurance:** confirm severity levels for all software non-conformances are defined; assess the application and accuracy of severity levels on non-conformances; confirm severity levels are assigned to non-conformances in tools, COTS, GOTS, MOTS, OSS and reused components; maintain or access the number of non-conformances at each severity level for each software configuration item (NASA-STD-8739.8B tasks 1 to 4, as listed). ## SWE-203 Mandatory Assessments for Non-Conformances

%guidance[HB-203 interprets=SWE-203] For every COTS, GOTS, MOTS, OSS or reused component, including operating systems, run-time systems, device drivers, code generators, compilers, math libraries and build and CM tools (NPR note), the project gathers the known non-conformance lists (vendor portals, OSS trackers, release notes, prior project reports) and assesses each for impact in the NASA system context, pre-flight, with mandatory code audits for critical defects (NPR note). - **Software assurance:** confirm evaluations of reported non-conformances for all COTS, GOTS, MOTS, OSS or reused components occur throughout the life cycle; assess the impact of non-conformances on the project software's safety, quality and reliability (NASA-STD-8739.8B; no task numbers given). ## SWE-204 Process Assessments

%guidance[HB-204 interprets=SWE-204] For every high-severity non-conformance (high severity defined by the project with engineering and SA, consistent with SWE-202), the project performs a root cause analysis (problem statement, event timeline, separating root causes from causal and contributing factors with fishbone, cause-effect or 5 Whys methods, continued until organizational factors are found or data run out), assesses the processes that allowed the defect, and takes corrective actions for both the defect and the process. The loop is closed by tracking corrective actions to closure, verifying their effectiveness through audits, retests and recurrence metrics, applying fixes to similar code or requirements, and recording lessons learned.

- **Objective evidence:** RCA reports (problem, impacts, root causes, method used, supporting data such as anomaly logs and timelines); NCRs marked high severity with impact assessment; grouped RCA documentation where applicable; corrective action logs (ID, dates, owner, status, severity); closure records and verification reports; regression test results after corrective actions; corrective action trend metrics; process assessment reports; process gap documentation; improvement recommendations; updated process artifacts (procedures, templates, checklists, standards); audit reports; process review logs; defect rate metrics over time; lessons learned reports; management review sign-offs; high-severity non-conformance tracking logs; defect attribution data by process or phase; summary non-conformance reports; life-cycle phase metrics; meeting minutes; RCA and corrective action review records; approval records
- **Documents:** RCA reports; corrective action log; process improvement status reports; project lessons learned repository; SA audit reports
- **Milestones:** effectiveness of corrective actions verified at routine milestone reviews (small projects: delivery or testing phases, sprint retrospectives); no specific life-cycle review named for the main process
- **Small projects:** Agree early what "high severity" means (critical, major, non-critical), do rigorous RCA only for high-severity issues tied to safety, mission or reliability, and use a short RCA template (title and severity, cause summary, root causes, contributing factors, corrective actions). Assess only the process areas involved, fold assessments into existing reviews or retrospectives, and track a few metrics (high-severity count, time to closure, recurrence rate, root causes addressed).
- **Software assurance:** perform or confirm an RCA on all high-severity non-conformances, with results recorded and assessed for adequacy; confirm the project analyzed the processes identified by the RCA; assess improvement opportunities for those processes; perform or confirm tracking of corrective actions to closure (NASA-STD-8739.8B tasks 1 to 4, as listed). SA products: RCA reports; record of corrective action closures; process improvement status reports; SA audit reports; lessons learned documentation.
- **Also:** guidance gives an RCA definitions table: cause (causal factor), proximate cause, root cause, RCA, event, condition, organizational factors, contributing factor, barrier; root cause check: would removing it prevent recurrence, and does evidence point to a process-level failure; high-severity non-conformances may be analyzed as a group or individually.

## SWE-205 Determination of Safety-Critical Software

%guidance[HB-205 interprets=SWE-205] The project manager and the SMA organization jointly decide, for each software component, whether it is safety-critical under NASA-STD-8739.8B section 4.2: software is safety-critical when, traceable to a hazard analysis, it causes or contributes to a hazardous condition or event, controls functions named in a system hazard, mitigates a hazardous condition, mitigates damage after one, or detects, reports and corrects a potentially hazardous state, with other independent protections (hardware, barriers, administrative) considered in the hazard definition. The determination starts in Formulation from high-level hazard and risk analyses and is reassessed as CSCIs, interfaces, models and simulations are defined, including changes to heritage software. Each safety-critical component then gets the added NASA-STD-8739.8 safety requirements (SWE-023, SWE-134), a tailored safety plan, and stricter design, verification and validation.

- **Objective evidence:** safety-critical software determination worksheet (criteria used, list of components evaluated, safety-critical or not per component, PM and SMA signatures); hazard analysis report linking hazards to software components; software safety criticality checklist per component, signed by SMA lead and PM; risk management plan with safety-critical software risks and mitigations; software safety plan with classification results, alignment to NASA-STD-8739.8, testing and assurance plans and roles; SMA concurrence memorandum and review minutes; Technical Authority concurrence memos

- **Small projects:** Record classification and safety-criticality decisions in a simple spreadsheet or decision log with a small team (PM, software engineer, SMA representative), asking for each component whether failure could cause injury, mission failure or property damage, whether it mitigates hazards and whether it controls hazardous functions; any "yes" makes it safety-critical regardless of project size. - **Also:** definition of safety-critical (from NPR 8715.3): any condition, event, operation, process, equipment or system that could cause severe injury, major damage or mission failure if done or built wrong or left uncorrected; safety-critical software also identified from the Orbital Debris Assessment Report or end-of-mission plan where applicable, in flight, ground support, mission operations and test facilities; hazard analysis must consider software common-mode failures across redundant computers and must not let software violate the independence of hazard inhibits or hardware redundancy; SA tab gives a long checklist of software hazard cause questions (NASA-STD-8739.8 Appendix A) and a table of software cause areas (data, commanding, flight computer, OS, programmable logic, timing, coding and algorithm, fault management, process, human-machine interface, security, unknown unknowns), several citing cyclomatic complexity above 15 as a cause; early SA inputs: PHA, ConOps, generic hazard lists, critical items list, reliability assessments, risk assessments, RFP, computing system safety analyses, security assessment, science requirements document.

## SWE-206 Auto-Generation Software Inputs

## SWE-210 Detection of Adversarial Actions

%guidance[HB-210 interprets=SWE-210] The project identifies software requirements, tailored to the system's risk posture and approved by the appropriate authorities, for three things: collecting data that reveal adversarial actions (key observables such as failed logins, performance changes, internal communication changes, configuration changes, integrity check failures, boundary violations, unauthorized commands, built-in self-test results, anomalous resource use), reporting them (actionable reports with severity levels and context, adaptive thresholds to limit false positives, escalation of critical compromises, operator acknowledgment, periodic human log review), and storing them (independent, redundant, encrypted, tamper-evident storage with metadata, verified recovery states, sized against log-exhaustion denial of service, retained per NASA records retention). Collection methods are designed before deployment, including agents external to the monitored system; per the NPR note, raw event data are analyzed to judge whether an anomaly is an attack and its nature.

- **Objective evidence:** SRS with explicit collection, reporting and storage requirements traced to cybersecurity goals; RTM; derived and allocated requirements; architecture and design documents showing logging, reporting and secure storage; system and software design review artifacts; ICDs; data flow diagrams; test plans, procedures and scripts; test results and reports; penetration test reports; regression test reports; code implementing logging, alerting and secure storage; configuration files (log levels, rotation, alert triggers); VCS logs; security-focused code review records; requirements review records; risk assessment reports; SA reports; CM plans; operational logs; incident response reports; lessons learned
- **Documents:** SRS; RTM; architecture and design documents; ICDs; test plans and reports; CM plan; incident response plan (small projects)
- **Milestones:** requirements identified and reviewed during requirements definition (SA participates in software requirements reviews); design coverage confirmed at system and software design reviews; no other specific milestone named
- **Small projects:** Log only the critical events tied to the project's risks (failed logins with timestamps, unauthorized access, configuration changes, privileged commands) with system-native or open-source tools, alert by email or simple dashboard on high-severity events only, and store logs encrypted and checksummed in at least two locations separate from the primary system. The tab suggests keeping critical security logs 6 to 12 months, sizing log storage up front, reviewing logs weekly and responding per a lightweight incident response plan.
- **Software assurance:** confirm that software requirements exist for collecting, reporting and storing data relating to detection of adversarial actions (NASA-STD-8739.8B; single task, no number given). SA products: analysis of volatility of security-related requirements with a trend report; confirmation artifacts for task 1 (RTM linking detection requirements to functions and tests, gaps and corrective actions, notes from requirements reviews).
- **Also:** first used in NPR 7150.2D (reserved in rev B); SA checks that requirements say what to log, that logging anticipates log deletion or alteration, that reporting defines severity thresholds and escalation and detects undelivered alerts, that storage defines backup, redundancy and modification protection, and that detection is reflected in the system hazard analysis where safety-critical; rationale cites NASA-STD-1006, NIST SP 800-53, FISMA and ISO 27001.

## SWE-211 Test Levels of Non-Custom Developed Software

%guidance[HB-211 interprets=SWE-211] Embedded COTS, GOTS, MOTS, OSS and reused components are tested, verified and validated to the same level as custom-developed software, with project classification and criticality setting the effort. Testing is tied to the project requirements the component satisfies and covers only the features actually used (in the project environment, ideally the final operational hardware), with unused features disabled or shown safe by analysis and the rationale for untested features documented; black-box limits are handled through interface and integration testing and vendor test suites, OSS adds static analysis, code review and security evaluation, MOTS modifications are identified and tested along with interfaces, and heritage software is retested for the new environment rather than relying on past use. Specific versions are under CM and updates are regression tested before deployment; where full testing is infeasible, a documented risk assessment and mitigations are required.

- **Objective evidence:** requirements traceability matrix to component features and tests; software test plan for non-custom components; test procedures (interface, integration, regression, performance); safety test plan for safety-critical functions; test execution logs; test reports; regression test reports; performance test reports; interface validation reports; defect and non-conformance logs; root cause analysis reports for critical defects; software configuration index of versions and patch levels; test baseline identification; change requests for MOTS; hazard reports; software safety assessment; FMECA; vendor or community test results and their evaluation; security analysis and scans; stress test results; environment-specific and hardware-in-the-loop results; risk tracking reports; SA sign-off checklists; acceptance review records; OSS licensing compliance evidence; AI/ML validation evidence where applicable
- **Documents:** software test plan (5.10); test procedures; test reports; software assurance plan (V&V strategy for non-custom components); software configuration index; hazard reports
- **Milestones:** SA sign-off after test completion; acceptance review records; no other milestone named
- **Small projects:** Understand the component's design assumptions and original operating conditions, request vendor or prior-project test suites and rerun key functions on the project's own hardware, and test only the functions used (for example only the needed MIL-STD-1553 protocol handling), documenting why other features are excluded. Projects reusing the same CSCI across campaigns (for example repeated sounding rocket flights) can share test environments, procedures and a common regression repository; Class A or high-criticality components get independent verification and hazard analysis.
- **Software assurance:** confirm the project tests COTS, GOTS, MOTS, OSS or reused components to the same level as developed software for its intended use (NASA-STD-8739.8B; single task, no number given). ## SWE-219 Code Coverage for Safety Critical Software

%guidance[HB-219 interprets=SWE-219] If a project has safety-critical software, every identified safety-critical component must reach 100% test coverage under MC/DC: every entry and exit point invoked, every decision taking every outcome, every condition taking true and false, and each condition shown to independently affect its decision's outcome. MC/DC testing is done mainly in the unit test phase with coverage tools (for example gcov), with developers documenting compliance; any deviation from 100% is reviewed and waived with rationale under Technical Authority approval (NPR note as updated on the page). Reused and third-party safety items (COTS, GOTS, reused) need analysis of their new role, known issues and preconditions for safe use, and verification against the new system's safety requirements, with responsibility staying with the integrating project.

- **Objective evidence:** code coverage analysis report per safety-critical component (function, statement, branch or decision coverage, total vs 100%); RTM from safety-critical requirements to code to test cases, with uncovered code mapped to justifications; test plans and execution logs; coverage gap analysis and risk assessment report when under 100% (why untestable, likelihood, consequence, mitigations, SMA sign-off); IV&V reports on coverage and gap justifications; fault injection and stress testing evidence for uncovered code; SMA concurrence or waiver with Technical Authority or SMA signatures
- **Documents:** software test plan; RTM; coverage gap and risk report; waiver records; 7.21 multi-condition software requirements (MC/DC examples)
- **Milestones:** MC/DC performed during unit testing, incrementally; no specific life-cycle review named
- **Small projects:** The tab states the requirement applies to all Class A, B, C and D projects with safety-critical software regardless of size; limit MC/DC to safety-critical components, use tools such as gcov or VectorCAST, test incrementally at unit level, and keep tool coverage reports (a one-page summary of tool results can suffice). It walks through a two-condition example showing each condition's independent effect.
- **Software assurance:** confirm 100% code test coverage is addressed for all identified safety-critical components, or that developers provide a technically acceptable rationale or risk assessment explaining why coverage is not possible or why the risk does not justify the cost of more coverage (NASA-STD-8739.8B; single task, no number given). SA products: SA or SE status reports; software design analysis; software test analysis; source code quality analysis; evidence of test coverage, complexity and safety testing; risk assessment for gaps; validation that hazardous loaded and uplinked data, rules and scripts are tested.
- **Also:** first used in NPR 7150.2D; SA test coverage analysis has two parts, requirements-based test analysis and structural coverage analysis; under 100% requires documented gap, justification, SA risk assessment and discussion of alternative assurance (defensive design, fault tolerance); rationale compares MC/DC requirements in DO-178B/C, ISO 26262 ASIL D and IEC 61508-3; further reading NASA/TM-20205011566 (NESC-RP-20-01515, cyclomatic complexity and basis path testing study).

## SWE-220 Cyclomatic Complexity for Safety-Critical Software

%guidance[HB-220 interprets=SWE-220] If a project has safety-critical software, every identified safety-critical component must have a McCabe cyclomatic complexity of 15 or lower; any exceedance is reviewed and waived with rationale by the project manager or the technical approval authority. Developers measure complexity with static analysis tools during development, refactor modules over the limit into smaller ones, and for any component at 16 or above the project manager works with SA on a risk assessment covering testability, maintainability and code quality, documenting why the limit is exceeded. Per the NPR note, all safety-critical components scoring over 15 are assessed for testability, maintainability and code quality.

- **Objective evidence:** cyclomatic complexity analysis reports from tools (value per safety-critical component, exceedances flagged, measurement method); source code and complexity reduction evidence (before and after values, refactoring logs, change requests); RTM mapping safety-critical components to complexity values, actions and waiver status; waivers with justification, risk assessment and approval signatures (project manager or technical approval authority, SMA); risk assessment reports for high-complexity components; IV&V assessment reports for components over the threshold
- **Documents:** complexity analysis report; waiver records; risk assessment reports; SA status reports; software hazard reports; NPR 7150.2 and NASA-STD-8739.8 requirements mapping matrices signed by Engineering and SMA technical authorities
- **Milestones:** measured during development and testing with continuous monitoring; no specific life-cycle review named
- **Small projects:** The tab is general small-project engineering advice rather than specific to this requirement; it says to keep safety-critical code at 15 or lower even on small projects, refactor complex code into smaller testable units, use static analysis tools (for example SonarQube, Coverity) and validate all independent paths in unit and system tests.
- **Software assurance:** perform or analyze cyclomatic complexity metrics on all identified safety-critical components; confirm all have a value of 15 or lower, and otherwise assure that developers provide a technically acceptable risk assessment, accepted by the proper technical authority, explaining why the value must exceed 15 and why the component cannot be restructured or why the cost and risk of reducing it are not justified (NASA-STD-8739.8B tasks 1 and 2, as listed). SA products: SA status reports; software hazard reports; software design analysis; source code quality analysis; software complexity data; SA risk assessments; requirements mapping matrices signed by Engineering and SMA technical authorities.
- **Also:** first used in NPR 7150.2D; definition: number of linearly independent paths, computed as E - N + 2 on the control flow graph, also the minimum number of test cases for basis path coverage; guidance table: 1 to 10 well structured, 10 to 15 manageable for safety-critical, 15 to 20 needs thorough review, 20 to 40 very complex, over 40 unsuitable for safety-critical; source of the 15 limit is the NIST Structured Testing methodology ("limit to 15 or explain in writing"); references NASA/TM-20205011566 and NESC-RP-20-01515.

## SWE-021 Transition to a Higher Class

%guidance[HB-021 interprets=SWE-021] the project manager (NPR 2.2.8); binds projects, not an institution, despite sitting in chapter 2. - **Also:** For the project: the project keeps a contributor log (in version control or a standalone document) with each contributor's name, role, employer or affiliation (civil servant, contractor, academic) and type of contribution, plus COTS and open-source components with source and license, updated at releases, new personnel and new third-party integrations; it places the standard disclaimer prominently (README, user guides, source header comments) and checks export control status with the Center Legal Office. Required disclaimer content (NPR): the software may be subject to U.S. export control restrictions; it is provided "as is" without any warranty, express or implied; the recipient waives any claims against, and indemnifies and holds harmless, NASA and its contractors and subcontractors. The Guidance gives a standard wording of exactly that text. POC checklist: complete contributor list including third parties; disclaimer in all documentation; rights, licenses and legal constraints reviewed and documented; export control checked; Legal Office consulted for work with partners or contractors. New in Rev C. No Objective Evidence tab.

## SWE-218 Contracting Officers

%guidance[HB-218 interprets=SWE-218] Contracting Officers (FAR 2.101) or Agreement Managers (NAII 1050.3), together with Program/Project Managers (NPR 2.1.7.1). Every NASA contract, Space Act Agreement, cooperative agreement, partnership agreement, grant or other agreement under which software is acquired, developed, modified, operated or managed for NASA includes the appropriate FAR, NFS and other clauses based on NPR 7150.2 and NASA-STD-8739.8. In practice the project works with the CO early (acquisition planning) to pick data-rights clauses, define the Government's rights (unlimited versus limited or restricted), specify allowed markings, list all software deliverables including source code in the SOW and CDRLs with acceptance standards, and flow terms down to subcontractors.

- **Software assurance:** (no 7.1 tasking table; SA guidance only) review contract and agreement drafts with CO, Agreement Manager and PM; verify FAR/NFS software and data-rights clauses are present; verify agreements require compliance with NASA-STD-8739.8 and NPR 7150.2, metrics, testing and V&V, risk analyses, safety and criticality, and delivery of design reports, test results and hazard analyses; check tailored provisions are documented and justified; spot-check active agreements and report missing clauses; verify licensing, IP and export control (ITAR/EAR) terms with legal review for international agreements.
- **Also:** For the project: the program/project manager shares responsibility with the CO for the clauses; the project defines in the SOW and CDRLs every software and data deliverable (source code, documentation), the rights it needs, acceptance criteria, and the NPR 7150.2 and NASA-STD-8739.8 requirements flowed to the supplier; it avoids limited-rights terms for key mission software unless necessary. Clauses named: FAR 52.227-14 Rights in Data General (include in every software contract that generates, furnishes or acquires data; Alt II limited rights notice for technical data; Alt III restricted rights notice for software; Alt IV contractor copyright with Government sponsorship notice); FAR 52.227-15 limited rights data and restricted software; FAR 52.227-16 additional data requirements; FAR 52.227-17 special works (with NFS 1852.227-17); FAR 52.227-19 commercial computer software license; FAR 12.212 and 27.405-3 for commercial software; NFS 1852.227-14 NASA rights in data; NFS 1852.227-19; NFS 1852.227-86 commercial software licensing (SA tab); NFS 1852.227-88 Government-furnished computer software (use only for the contract, return modified source code, as-is, waiver and indemnification flowed to subcontractors); an H-clause can require copyright assignment to NASA. The page reproduces the full texts of FAR 12.212, 27.405-3, NFS 1852.227-19, the limited and restricted rights notices, and NFS 1852.227-88. The SA tab miscites "NASA-STD-7150.2" for the NPR. New in Rev D. No Objective Evidence tab.

## SWE-221 OSMA NPR Appraisals

%guidance[HB-221 interprets=SWE-221] NASA Chief, SMA (NPR 2.1.2.5); OSMA runs the audits with the NASA Safety Center; Centers and projects support them. OSMA authorizes compliance appraisals against selected NPR 7150.2 requirements, typically as part of a QAAR audit of a Center's or project's processes, directives and official records, covering software engineering, assurance, safety and IV&V, plus NPD 7120.4E and NASA-STD-8739.8. Audits look at the life-cycle framework, review structure, TA implementation, software risk, dissent and waiver or deviation handling, SA and safety management, systems engineering integration, lessons learned and technical standards, and specifically at safety-critical determination and tailoring, SA plans, resources and metrics, access to software products, cybersecurity, coding standards and tools, hazards, IV&V, and reuse and open source.

- **Software assurance:** 7.1 tasking: none identified; no SA products or metrics specified; 7.4 guidance lists SA focus areas during appraisals.
- **Also:** For the project: the audited project supplies documents and access and answers findings; typical requests: current SA plan; SDP/SMP; IV&V Project Execution Plan and findings; NPR 7150.2 and SA requirements mapping matrices; latest SA and software engineering metrics and status reports and known issues; current list of software hazards, risks and issues; verification and test plans; list of open-source and reused software with the approach; coding standards; software quality assessments; description of the document management system. Finding categories: strengths; weaknesses (need improvement, not non-compliant); observations; opportunities; non-compliances (violations that affect safety, quality or mission success). Significant failures or safety concerns go at once to the organization's management; all findings and corrective actions go in the final audit report. The Rationale says SWE-129 "is written from the OSMA point of view", apparently meaning this SWE. New in Rev D (earlier a "will" statement). No Objective Evidence tab.

## SWE-223 Tailoring IV&V project selections

%guidance[HB-223 interprets=SWE-223] NASA Chief, SMA (NPR 2.1.2.7). Only the Chief, SMA makes the final decision on any proposed tailoring of SWE-141 (software IV&V). A project's tailoring request is reviewed by the NASA IV&V Advisory Board (IAB), which recommends to the Chief, SMA, checking that technical, managerial and financial independence and SMA goals are preserved. The IAB also reviews the scope of IV&V services annually against budget, which feeds the IV&V Facility's scope of work.

- **Software assurance:** 7.1 tasking: none identified; no SA products or metrics specified; 7.4 guidance has SA evaluate the need for tailoring, prepare tailoring documentation, support the Chief, SMA review, implement the decision and follow up.
- **Also:** For the project: a project that wants to tailor SWE-141 documents and justifies the request: software classification, risks and mission criticality, trade-offs (resource reductions and mitigations for uncovered areas), and evidence that tailoring will not compromise performance, safety or reliability; the small-projects tab adds coordinating with the Center SMA organization before submission to the IAB; the agreed IV&V approach is captured in the IV&V Project Execution Plan (IPEP). IV&V independence has three parts: technical (analysts not involved in development), managerial (provider reports outside the development organization) and financial (funded outside the project budget). The Katherine Johnson IV&V Facility is the sole IV&V provider when SWE-141 applies. The Mission Directorate Associate Administrator may extend IV&V to projects that do not meet SWE-141 criteria, and IV&V may be required by the AA or the Chief, SMA for other phases or projects without a payload risk classification. IV&V provider requirements are in NASA-STD-8739.8. New in Rev D (earlier a "will" statement). No Objective Evidence tab.
